\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl-conclusion}

This report addressed a gap between CDSCO's batch-level quality alerts and the medicine pack in a
person's hand. The design follows from two properties measured in the alert data: batch numbers
collide across unrelated products, and listed batches have confusable siblings from the same
manufacturer. A batch match alone therefore cannot justify an alarm, and a confident OCR reading
cannot justify a miss. StripCheck answers with a bounded agent that searches for likely misreads,
grades every candidate field by field, asks the person to read the characters in doubt before any
alarm, and lets a rule-based verifier downgrade any draft the evidence does not support. A local
model can pick every tool in a ReAct loop under rule-computed allowed tools, a rejected draft is revised once, four
agents hand work to each other, and a vision reading needs the person's confirmation. Every loop is bounded and every
model call has a fallback, so the whole check runs offline on a laptop.

On the evaluation set of \res{ResN} rendered packs, with a person answering the questions, the
agent removed the baseline's collision and sibling false alarms (\res{ResRulesOracleFARCollisionK}
and \res{ResRulesOracleFARSiblingK} against \res{ResBaseOracleFARCollisionK} and
\res{ResBaseOracleFARSiblingK}) and raised hit recall from \res{ResBaseOracleHitRecall} to
\res{ResRulesOracleHitRecall} at \res{ResRulesOracleQuestions} questions per pack. Without answers
it abstained on \res{ResRulesSkipAbstainK} packs and still raised no false alarm, so the questions
carry the recall. The hybrid and tool-calling planners gave the same verdict metrics. The first run reached
the same decision with a hit recall of \RunOneRulesOracleHitRecall; three general defects found in
it were fixed before the final run, so the figures are optimistic and the set is no longer a blind test. Table~\ref{tab:concl-objectives} revisits each objective of
Section~\ref{sec:intro-objectives}.

On these packs the gain over the lookup came from the deterministic parts of the agent, since
neither the hybrid nor the tool-calling planner changed a verdict. The vision fallback never triggered in the evaluation. The planned ablation of the confirmation gate was not run. The system stays advisory throughout: a \NLISTED{} result states the lists loaded and that it does not certify the medicine, and every alarm directs the person to a pharmacist.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future-work}

Each item follows from a limitation in Chapter~\ref{ch:lim}.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Real packs and a held-out set.} All evaluation packs are rendered images and the
seed was run twice with fixes in between. A fresh seed with photos of real strips and
cartons under varied light would measure OCR errors instead of simulating them.
\item \emph{A study with people.} The oracle answers every question correctly, while real users may
misread a character or skip the question, and the recall depends on those answers. A usability
study should measure how often and how accurately people answer, and whether they understand that
\NLISTED{} does not certify a medicine.
\item \emph{Wider list coverage.} The index starts in January 2019. An adapter for the earlier PDF
months and for state-level feeds would extend the coverage that every \NLISTED{} result depends on.
\item \emph{Regional languages.} Verdicts are in English only, because no Hindi or Kannada template has been
reviewed by native speakers; reviewed templates and voice prompts would serve users who do not read English.
\item \emph{An independent second reader and a pharmacy workflow.} The local vision model is
only a fallback that the person must confirm, slow on the CPU, with unreliable confidence. Real photographs, a GPU and
a pharmacist workflow that checks a whole delivery would move the check earlier.
\end{enumerate}

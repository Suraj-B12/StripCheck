\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

This report describes StripCheck, an agent that checks whether a medicine pack in a person's hand
comes from a batch that India's drug regulator has listed as failing a quality test or as spurious.
The chapter sets out the regulatory process in which the problem arises, states the decision the
agent supports, explains why the task calls for an agent with tools, a critic and a person in the
loop, and fixes the objectives and scope against which the later chapters are measured.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:intro-background}

Substandard and falsified medicines harm patients in every region, and low- and middle-income
countries (LMICs) carry most of the burden~\cite{ozawa2018prevalence}. Most failures are
substandard batches from licensed manufacturers (Section~\ref{sec:lit-existing-practice-in-the-sector}),
and such a batch looks correct in every respect, so packaging cues give the holder no warning.

In India, central and state drug-testing laboratories test samples drawn from the market. The
Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) publishes the batches that fail as monthly
alerts of drugs Not of Standard Quality (NSQ) and of spurious drugs~\cite{cdsco2026portal}.
Until mid-2025 the alerts were monthly PDF tables;
since August 2025 they appear in a month-by-month web viewer whose JavaScript Object Notation
(JSON) endpoints serve every month back to January 2019~\cite{cdsco2025nsqlink,cdsco2026nsqpage,cdsco2026portal}.
The volume of alerts has grown quickly. Figure~\ref{fig:intro-alerts-per-year}
counts the alerts in our local index by year of the list month: 399 in 2019, 1,914 in 2025, and
1,897 in the first nine months of 2026.

A listed batch usually stays in circulation. CDSCO's recall guidelines carry a recall down to the
consumer only for the most serious class of defect~\cite{cdsco2024recall}, so for most listed
batches the published list is the only signal a patient or a pharmacist can reach. In the portal
snapshot of \SnapshotDate{} the median batch is listed \LagMedian{} months after manufacture,
when it still has \ShelfMedian{} months of shelf life left; \ShelfTwelvePlusPct\% of listed batches
have at least a year left and only \ExpiredAtAlertPct\% have expired. Packaging codes do not close
this gap: the Quick Response (QR) code that the top 300 brands carry since August 2023 identifies
the pack and carries no test result, so a batch that a licensed manufacturer made badly carries a
valid code, and existing medicine apps do not check the regulator's lists
(Section~\ref{sec:lit-existing-practice-in-the-sector}).

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:intro-problem-statement}

A patient, a caregiver or a pharmacist holding a strip, bottle or carton cannot tell, at the point
of use, whether that pack belongs to a batch on a CDSCO alert list. The input is what the pack
shows: one or two photos of the printed batch line, dates, product and manufacturer, or the same
details typed by hand. The reference is the set of monthly NSQ and spurious lists, which are tables
of product names, batch numbers, dates and manufacturers. Comparing the two by hand means finding
the right months, reading a batch code from foil and matching it character by character against
thousands of rows. Two errors are possible and both matter. A missed listed batch leaves the holder
without a warning the regulator has already issued, and a false alarm on an unlisted batch can
frighten a patient away from a prescribed medicine. The output must therefore be one of a small
set of advisory verdicts, each tied to its evidence (Table~\ref{tab:intro-verdicts}). The verdict
labels are internal codes used in the code, traces and tables; the person sees only the fixed
template headlines, and for \SFAKE{} the headline reads ``This batch is listed as purported
spurious''.
\textbf{The decision supported is: is this exact pack from a batch on a CDSCO alert list, and if the
evidence cannot settle that, what must the person confirm?}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{The four verdicts and the condition under which each is returned}
\label{tab:intro-verdicts}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
Verdict & Returned when \\
\midrule
\FLAGGED & The confirmed batch matches an NSQ entry, and the product together with the dates or the
maker corroborates it. \\

\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\section{Motivation and Why Agentic AI}
\label{sec:intro-motivation-and-why-agentic-ai}

Automating the check looks like a lookup, and the simplest program reads the batch, normalises it
and searches the lists. Our evaluation includes exactly this program as a baseline. It fails in two
ways, and both are measurable in the CDSCO data.

\emph{Reading.} Optical character recognition (OCR) on blister foil makes confident
single-character errors. In our tests a scuffed \texttt{B} was read as \texttt{3} with a reported
confidence of 1.0, and an enhanced re-read returned the same string, so the reader's confidence
cannot settle a character (Section~\ref{sec:lit-related-technical-work}). A misread batch either
misses a listed batch or lands on a different one.

\emph{Matching.} Batch numbers are short and each manufacturer chooses its own, so one string can
appear on unrelated products. In the portal snapshot, \CollisionKeys{} batch keys are shared by
products with no name token in common, \NeighbourKeys{} keys (\NeighbourKeysPct\%) have another
listed key one look-alike substitution away, and \SiblingKeys{} of these (\SiblingKeysPct\% of all keys)
share the manufacturer, as in a pair of the form \texttt{AB1119} and \texttt{AB1719} (a schematic
example; the report prints no batch string from an alert row). Manufacturers number batches
in sequence and the regulator tests only samples, so an unlisted sibling of a listed batch can sit
on the same shelf as the listed one. A confident misread can land exactly on a listed sibling, and
every other field on the pack will then agree with the alert.

These two properties rule out the simpler designs. A lookup script raises an alarm on any batch
hit, so it alarms on every collision and on every misread that lands on a sibling, and it misses
every listed batch that the reader got wrong. A fixed pipeline that always runs the same steps
cannot follow what the previous tool returned: an empty exact search calls for a variant search, a
variant hit for a question about one character, and weak evidence for abstention. A single prompt to a large
language model (LLM) cannot read the pack reliably, cannot query thousands of alert rows, cannot
ask the person a question and wait for the answer, and gives no guarantee that its wording stays
within what the evidence supports; language models also rarely abstain on medical
questions~\cite{cocchieri2026abstain}.

The task therefore needs a small set of agentic capabilities, each tied to a property of the
problem (Table~\ref{tab:intro-capabilities}). The loop follows the reason-and-act pattern of
interleaving decisions with tool calls~\cite{yao2023react}, kept to the simple, composable form
that practitioner guidance recommends~\cite{schluntz2024building}. The critic is external and
rule-based, in line with the LLM-Modulo argument that a model's plans should be checked by sound
external verifiers~\cite{kambhampati2024llmmodulo}. The planner that chooses among the allowed actions is a
deterministic rule set by default; an optional local LLM can make the choice at two branch points,
and any failure falls back to the rule choice. The agentic structure lies in the loop, the tools,
the verifier and the questions, and it works with or without the model.

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:intro-objectives}

The project set six objectives, revisited in Section~\ref{sec:concl-conclusion}.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item To build a provenance-keeping ingest of the CDSCO portal that stores every raw snapshot,
validates and quarantines rows, and indexes the alerts from January 2019 onward, and to measure in
these data the batch collisions, confusable siblings and alert timing that motivate the design.
\item To design and implement a bounded plan-act-observe agent that searches the index by exact and
confusion-aware batch keys, grades every candidate by record linkage, and completes every check
offline, with no LLM and no network, within 16 steps and two questions.
\item To implement a human-in-the-loop confirmation gate, so that no alarm is raised on a batch read
only by OCR, together with a rule-based verifier that downgrades any unsupported draft to an
abstention.
\item To instrument every check with a step-level trace, recording for each step the action, its
source (rules, LLM, fallback, verifier or user) and a short rationale, with optional export to a
local tracing server.
\item To evaluate the agent against a lookup baseline on 60 rendered packs under a decision rule
stated before the run (Section~\ref{sec:exp-hypothesis}).
\item To compare, in an A/B experiment, the rule planner with a hybrid planner that consults a local
LLM at branch points, under a second stated decision rule.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the Project}
\label{sec:intro-scope-of-the-project}

\paragraph{Included.} StripCheck covers the NSQ and spurious alerts that CDSCO publishes through
its portal, from \FirstMonth{} to the latest month synced (\LastMonth{} at the time of the
evaluation). A check starts from one or two photos of a pack or from typed details. The agent
returns one of the four verdicts in Table~\ref{tab:intro-verdicts}, each alarm cites the list,
month, row and stored source of the entry, and every check runs on a laptop without a network
connection, a graphics processor or a paid service. The system includes web and command-line
interfaces, an iOS client tested in continuous integration only, a cabinet of saved packs that is
re-checked after each sync, and an evaluation harness.

\paragraph{Excluded.} StripCheck does not assess the quality of a medicine. A \NLISTED{} result
means only that no matching entry was found on the lists loaded, and the verdict text says so.
The system flags only a listed batch and never a brand, and it gives no medical advice beyond
showing the pack to a pharmacist. It is a student project and not a CDSCO service. Lists published
only as PDF before 2019 and state-level feeds are outside the index. A vision-language second
reader and reviewed Hindi and Kannada verdict text were planned but not built. The evaluation uses
synthetic packs rendered from real alert rows; no photos of real medicine strips were taken, so no
claim is made about accuracy on real packs.

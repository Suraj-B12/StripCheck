\chapter{Experimentation}
\label{ch:exp}

The evaluation of Chapter~\ref{ch:eval} doubles as a controlled experiment with two questions. The
first is whether the agentic layer (confusion-aware search, record-linkage grading, questions and
the verifier) is worth its cost compared with a plain lookup. The second is whether letting a local
language model choose the action at the branch points improves on the rule planner. This chapter
states the hypotheses and decision rules, the setup, the outcome on both seeds and on both runs of
the evaluation seed, and the configuration decision that followed.

\section{Hypothesis}
\label{sec:exp-hypothesis}

Both hypotheses and their decision rules were written into the design specification and stated at
Review~2, before the evaluation run, following the practice of preregistration~\cite{nosek2018preregistration}.
The decision log has no separately dated freeze entry for them, which we note as a limitation of
the registration.

\paragraph{H1 (agent against lookup, rule R-A).} With a person answering its questions, the agent
lowers the false-alarm rate on collision packs by at least 50\% relative to the lookup, while its hit
recall is no more than five percentage points lower. The rule is falsifiable on the collision and
listed packs of the evaluation set, and it is coded in \texttt{core/evaluate.py} as
$1-\mathrm{FAR}_{\text{agent}}/\mathrm{FAR}_{\text{lookup}}\geq 0.5$ and
$R_{\text{lookup}}-R_{\text{agent}}\leq 0.05$. The registered prediction adds four specific
outcomes per scenario, listed in Table~\ref{tab:exp-predictions}.

\paragraph{H2 (hybrid against rule planner, rule R-C).} The hybrid planner becomes the default only
if its accuracy is not significantly lower than the rule planner's (exact McNemar test,
$\alpha=0.05$) and its median latency is at most twice the rule planner's. Both conditions are
necessary; together they permit the change without requiring it. A further check, that the hybrid
planner does better on packs with a partial product match, was registered as exploratory.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-experimental-setup}

Table~\ref{tab:exp-setup} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp} lists what was varied and what was held
fixed. The arms are those of Section~\ref{sec:eval-baselines}, under the oracle and no-answer
policies. The lookup and the agent differ in the whole reasoning layer, so H1 compares two systems;
perception (read once per image and cached), index, packs, templates, verifier rules and loop bounds
are shared, so any difference comes from the reasoning between the read batch and the verdict. H2
changes a single factor: the planner at the two branch points. The rule arms are deterministic, so
the variation that matters is the choice of packs, which we probe with a second seed of the
generator. Seed~7 was run twice (run~1 at commit \texttt{\RunOneCommit}, run~2 after decision D25)
and seed~1 once with the run-2 code, all on one Windows~11 laptop; the model ran on the GPU in the
seed-1 run (Ollama log), and the device for the seed-7 runs was not recorded.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:exp-results}

\paragraph{H1 and rule R-A.} With the oracle user the agent with the rule planner cut collision false
alarms from \res{ResBaseOracleFARCollisionK} to \res{ResRulesOracleFARCollisionK}, a 100\% relative
reduction, and raised hit recall from \res{ResBaseOracleHitRecallK} to \res{ResRulesOracleHitRecallK}.
The exact McNemar test on per-pack correctness found \res{ResRulesOracleMcNemarB} packs that only the
agent got right and \res{ResRulesOracleMcNemarC} that only the lookup got right
($p={}$\res{ResRulesOracleMcNemarP}). The decision under R-A is \textbf{\res{ResRulesOracleDecision}}.
The hybrid arm gives the same counts. With every question skipped, hit recall falls to
\res{ResRulesSkipHitRecallK}, sixty points below the lookup, and the decision is
\res{ResRulesSkipDecision} ($p={}$\res{ResRulesSkipMcNemarP}, with \res{ResRulesSkipMcNemarB}
agent-only and \res{ResRulesSkipMcNemarC} lookup-only correct packs). The agent's benefit therefore
holds only when a person answers.

\paragraph{H2 and rule R-C.} The hybrid and rule planners returned identical labels on all
\res{ResN} packs under both policies, so there is no discordant pair and accuracy is not lower. The
median latency was \res{ResHybridOracleLatMedian}\,s against \res{ResRulesOracleLatMedian}\,s, about
1.1 times, within the factor of two. R-C therefore holds. The cost appears in the tail
(Figure~\ref{fig:eval-latency}, Appendix~\ref{app:supp}): the 95th percentile was \res{ResHybridOracleLatPNinetyFive}\,s
against \res{ResRulesOracleLatPNinetyFive}\,s and the mean \res{ResHybridOracleLatMean}\,s against
\res{ResRulesOracleLatMean}\,s, from \res{ResHybridOracleLLMSteps} model calls with
\res{ResHybridOracleFallbackSteps} fallbacks. The model's choices changed no verdict on these packs.
The exploratory check on
partial product matches was not evaluable, because no pack of seed~7 produced one.

\paragraph{Spread across seeds.} Figure~\ref{fig:exp-seeds} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp} repeats the headline metrics on the
development set (seed~1), run for this report with the same code as run~2. The agent arms give the
same counts on both sets: hit recall 40/40, accuracy 60/60 and no false alarm. The lookup differs by
one pack in hit recall (31/40 against \res{ResBaseOracleHitRecallK}) and in accuracy (39/60 against
\res{ResBaseOracleAccuracyK}). This is a net difference of two effects: on seed~1 the reader
returned a batch different from the rendered text on 4 of 60 images (60 of 60 on seed~7), which
cost the lookup two listed packs, while the lookup found the listed batch on one misread pack. The paired test on seed~1 gives 21 agent-only
and 0 lookup-only correct packs ($p=10^{-6}$), so R-A again gives adopt; without answers the agent
reaches 7/40 hit recall with 37/60 abstentions. The hybrid planner again has a long tail: a median of
1.25\,s against 1.19\,s for rules and a 95th percentile of 9.63\,s against 1.76\,s. The two
seeds are not a like-for-like latency comparison, because the model ran on the GPU in the seed-1
run and the device for the seed-7 runs was not recorded. Seed~1 is the
development set, so it shows the size of the variation between generated sets; it is not an
independent test.

\paragraph{Run 1 and run 2.} The evaluation seed was run twice, and every table in
Chapter~\ref{ch:eval} reports the second run. Run~1 (commit \texttt{\RunOneCommit}) used the same
packs, arms and rules and also gave \RunOneRulesOracleDecision{} under R-A: the rule agent reached hit
recall \RunOneRulesOracleHitRecallK{} with \RunOneRulesOracleFARCollisionK{} collision false alarms,
and McNemar gave $p=\RunOneRulesOracleMcNemarP$ (\RunOneRulesOracleMcNemarB{} agent-only,
\RunOneRulesOracleMcNemarC{} lookup-only). Two parts of the prediction failed: the agent was correct on
\RunOneRulesOracleCleanK{} clean packs and on \RunOneRulesOracleMisreadK{} misread packs.
Figure~\ref{fig:exp-runs} sets the two runs side by side and Table~\ref{tab:exp-predictions} gives
the prediction outcomes for every run.

The \RunOneRulesOracleMisses{} alarms the agent missed in run~1 were all on cartons that print a brand
line above the generic name. Their traces showed three defects, recorded as decision D25.
(1)~The investigator passed only the product and brand fields to grading, so a brand line could hide
the generic name and a listed batch was rejected as a collision; grading now sees the full product
text the reader returned. (2)~Verifier rule R2 required a full product match while grading accepts a
partial match when dates and maker both agree, so a person could answer every question and still
receive \CANTV; R2 now accepts what grading accepts. (3)~The batch confirmation asked only the first
two look-alike positions, so a misread at a third position could confirm the wrong batch; it now asks
every look-alike position. Each fix changes a general rule of the engine and none refers to a pack,
batch or threshold of the evaluation set. They were still made after we had seen run~1 on the
evaluation seed, so run~2 is no longer a held-out test and its numbers are optimistic. A fresh seed
and photographs of real packs are needed for an unbiased estimate.

\section{Discussion and Decision}
\label{sec:exp-discussion-and-decision}

H1 was supported on both seeds and on both runs of the evaluation seed: R-A gave adopt each time, and
the collision false alarms fell from all of them to none. The benefit depends on the person
answering, since without answers the agent abstains on most listed packs. We judged that trade
acceptable for this task. An abstention says that more evidence is needed, while a false alarm on a
collision accuses a batch of the wrong product. We therefore adopted the agent in place of the lookup
in every client and kept the questions mandatory before any alarm. The lookup survives only as
baseline arm~A.

H2 was also supported in the sense of the rule: R-C held on both seeds. The rule permits the hybrid
planner as the default and does not require it, and on these packs the model changed no verdict
while it raised the 95th-percentile latency from under 2\,s to about 9\,s. We therefore kept the rule
planner as the default (decision D26, \texttt{STRIPCHECK\_PLANNER=rules}). The hybrid planner stays
available per check, and its written reason for each branch decision helps a reviewer follow the
traces of Chapter~\ref{ch:eval}.

The limits of the experiment (small scenarios, an optimistic run~2, rendered packs and
over-represented traps) are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:lim-limitations}; a third seed generated
after the code is frozen and a set of real photographs are the next tests.

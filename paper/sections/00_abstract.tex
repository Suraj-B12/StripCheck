\begin{abstract}
India's Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) publishes monthly lists of drug samples that failed quality tests or were declared spurious. The lists name individual batches, yet a person holding a strip of tablets has no practical way to check them. StripCheck is an offline agent that reads a medicine pack, from a photo or typed details, and decides whether its batch appears on these lists. Two properties of the problem shape the design. Optical character recognition (OCR) misreads single characters with high confidence, so a batch number read from foil cannot be trusted alone. Batch numbers also collide across unrelated products, and sibling batches of one product often differ by a single character, so a matching batch number is weak evidence on its own. StripCheck retrieves candidates by exact and confusion-aware batch search, corroborates each candidate field by field in the manner of record linkage, asks the person to confirm a doubtful character or the batch itself before it raises any alarm, and sends every draft verdict through a rule-based verifier that can abstain. Every loop is bounded and every model call has a deterministic fallback, so a check completes with no language model and no network. The alert index is built from CDSCO's online portal: \NumAlertRows{} published rows from \FirstMonth{} to \LastMonth{}, of which \res{ResAlertCount} remain as indexed alerts after cleaning, de-duplication and quarantine. In these data the median alert appears \LagMedian{} months after manufacture, when \ShelfTwelvePlusPct\% of the flagged batches still have at least a year of shelf life left.

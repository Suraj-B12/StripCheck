# Reading strips against India's failed-batch lists

*Literature survey for StripCheck, covering work published up to October 2026. Citations use the BibTeX keys from `research_notes/StripCheck literature survey/*.bib` as link text, with the DOI or URL as the link target. Only sources the notes' verification logs mark VERIFIED or CORRECTED are cited.*

No published work or deployed tool found up to October 2026 answers StripCheck's question, whether the exact batch on a photographed strip is on a regulator's list of failed samples, read reliably enough to act on, although every ingredient of an answer exists. The question matters because most poor-quality medicine is substandard rather than falsified (about six of every seven failures in LMIC meta-analyses; roughly 3% NSQ against under 0.5% spurious or adulterated in India's own testing), substandard batches carry genuine packaging and valid codes, and only a laboratory finds them, so CDSCO's monthly batch-level NSQ and spurious lists are the one public quality signal a consumer can use. Yet India's recall rules reach consumers only for Class I recalls, which leaves households holding Class II and III batches outside every notification channel. Existing approaches answer neighbouring questions: QR codes, serialization and ledgers establish provenance; field spectrometers test chemistry but need devices, training and the right reference library; consumer apps identify pills or judge appearance; and the seven open projects built on CDSCO data in 2025-2026 are analytics pipelines, typed-batch lookups or a distributor-side agent, with the closest (Nazar) adding photo reading and an OCR look-alike guard but no field corroboration or human confirmation. The component literatures, from packaging OCR (the only pharmaceutical batch-code study found reports F1 of 80.9%) and VLM hallucination through noisy-channel correction, ALPR hotlist failures and Fellegi-Sunter record linkage to LLM-Modulo verifiers, selective prediction and residual-risk messaging, converge on one principle: confident errors are caught only by independent checks and an explicit option to abstain. StripCheck's defensible contribution is therefore an integration and a safety policy (confusion-aware search, field corroboration that separates collisions from spurious-pattern mismatches, a character-level question to the pack holder, a rule-based verifier, offline operation and a cabinet re-checked on every new list), together with measurements nobody has published: an annotated Indian strip dataset, a batch-code confusion matrix, OCR calibration, collision rates and the manufacture-to-listing lag. Novelty should be claimed as a combination, hedged against a fast-moving tool landscape, and NOT_LISTED must always read as "not listed as of this list", never as safe.

## 1. Most bad medicine is substandard, and India discloses it batch by batch

### 1.1 WHO's three SF categories map onto India's four statutory ones

The Seventieth World Health Assembly (May 2017) replaced the older "SSFFC" terminology with **"substandard and falsified (SF)" medical products** and adopted three working definitions: substandard products are authorised products that fail their quality standards or specifications, unregistered or unlicensed products have not been evaluated or approved for the market where they are sold, and falsified products deliberately or fraudulently misrepresent their identity, composition or source ([who2017definitions](https://www.who.int/teams/regulation-prequalification/incidents-and-SF/background/definitions)). India's Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 divides the same territory into four overlapping legal categories. A drug is **"not of standard quality" (NSQ)** when it fails the Second Schedule standard under s.16; misbranded under s.17; adulterated under s.17A; and **spurious under s.17B** when, among other things, it is manufactured under another drug's name, names a manufacturer that "is fictitious or does not exist", or "purports to be the product of a manufacturer of whom it is not truly a product" ([dcact1940s17b](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/drugsandcosmetics/17b.php)). NSQ corresponds roughly to WHO "substandard" and spurious to "falsified", but the Indian categories turn as much on who made a product and what its label claims as on its chemistry. The 2008 amendment widened the gap in consequences: offences involving spurious drugs now carry **seven years to life imprisonment** and are cognizable with restricted bail, while the residual clause under which ordinary NSQ falls, s.27(d), carries far lighter penalties, with a minimum fine of Rs 20,000 after the amendment ([goi2008dcamendment](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2008/the-drugs-and-cosmetics-%28amendment%29-act,-2008.pdf)). CDSCO's enforcement guideline then sorts Government Analyst reports into Category A (spurious and adulterated), Category B (grossly substandard, such as sterility, endotoxin or severe assay failures) and Category C (minor defects such as chipped tablets, discolouration or labelling errors) ([cdsco2008nsqguidelines](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadReportsFiles/Regulatory%20guidlines%20on%20taking%20action%20on%20NSQ%20drugs.pdf)).

Two design rules follow for any consumer-facing check. Because NSQ spans everything from a labelling slip to a non-sterile injectable, a positive verdict has to carry the regulator's category and stated reason for failure rather than a bare "bad" flag. And because a spurious listing is in effect an identity accusation against a named manufacturer, with criminal consequences, a spurious match needs stricter evidence than an NSQ match. StripCheck's verdict set encodes exactly this split.

| WHO term (WHA70, 2017) | Indian statutory category | Where CDSCO publishes it | StripCheck verdict on an exact batch match |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substandard | NSQ (s.16), from minor labelling defects to sterility failures; contamination can also be "adulterated" (s.17A) | Monthly central-laboratory and state-laboratory NSQ lists | FLAGGED, showing the regulator's failure reason |
| Falsified | Spurious (s.17B): another drug's name, a fictitious or false manufacturer, substitution | Monthly "List of spurious Drugs" | SUSPECTED_FAKE, only with manufacturer and brand corroboration |
| (no match, or evidence insufficient) | — | — | NOT_LISTED (never "safe") or CANT_VERIFY (abstain) |

### 1.2 One in eight samples fails in LMIC surveys; about three in a hundred fail India's official testing

The headline global figure comes from WHO's 2017 Global Surveillance and Monitoring System (GSMS) report, which estimated that **10.5% of medical products in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs)** are substandard or falsified, based on surveys covering about 48,000 samples in 88 LMICs, and modelled 72,000-169,000 child pneumonia deaths a year from SF antibiotics and 116,000 malaria deaths a year in sub-Saharan Africa from SF antimalarials ([who2017oneinten](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-11-2017-1-in-10-medical-products-in-developinG-countries-is-substandard-or-falsified)). WHO's 2024 fact sheet repeats the "1 in 10" estimate and an annual spend of US$30.5 billion on SF products ([who2024factsheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/substandard-and-falsified-medical-products)). Peer-reviewed meta-analyses land in the same range. Ozawa et al. pooled 96 studies (67,839 samples) to estimate **13.6% (95% CI 11.0-16.3%)** SF prevalence in LMICs, with 13.7% in Asia and 18.7% in Africa ([ozawa2018prevalence](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.1662)). A 2022 follow-up over 130 studies and 95,520 samples put essential-medicine failure at 12.4% and found that only 13.8% of failed samples had less than half the stated active ingredient, so roughly **six of every seven failures look substandard rather than falsified** ([ozawa2022characterizing](https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.21-1123)). The most recent meta-analysis, of 36,069 samples of maternal, newborn and child health medicines and antimalarials from 34 African countries, found 17.9% failing, with **substandard (12.8%) outnumbering falsified (1.2%) by about ten to one** ([rusatira2026nearly](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0006326)). Disease-specific reviews give similar rates, such as 15.2% of 7,682 anti-tuberculosis samples failing at least one test, which the authors caution is not generalisable to the global supply ([tabernero2023estimating](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2023-012039)). The spread is as telling as the means: a review of 33 sampling studies found a median prevalence of 25% (IQR 7.7-34%) and concluded that definitive prevalence cannot be drawn from such heterogeneous designs ([mcmanus2020systematic](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2020-002393)).

India's official figures are much lower. The National Drug Survey 2014-16, run by the National Institute of Biologicals on 47,012 samples of 224 molecules, reported **3.16% NSQ and 0.0245% spurious** as the Health Ministry's headline rates ([tnm2017nds](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/news/1850-drugs-sold-india-not-standard-quality-health-ministry-57646); [khatun2017nds](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/minister-admits-that-3-16-percent-of-drugs-in-india-not-of-standard-quality)), and a markedly higher **10.02% NSQ among samples drawn from government sources** ([singhania2019nds](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/more-than-10-percent-of-drug-samples-from-government-sources-not-of-standard-quality)). The raw counts published alongside (1,850 NSQ of 47,012 samples) give 3.94% rather than 3.16%, so the headline rates are probably weighted estimates whose method could not be checked; they are best cited as "as reported by the Ministry". Routine regulatory testing tells a consistent story at larger scale. Between 2020-21 and 2024-25 the samples tested each year rose from 84,874 to 116,323, and **14,342 samples were declared NSQ and 1,593 spurious or adulterated over the five years** ([roy2025fiveyears](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/over-14300-drug-samples-fail-quality-tests-in-five-years-minister-160877)).

These are failure rates among targeted regulatory samples, not population prevalence, and the literature does not explain why India's official rates sit about a factor of four below pooled LMIC estimates; sampling frames, laboratory capacity, definitions and under-detection are all candidate explanations. Two facts still matter for system design. Substandard quality dominates both globally and in India, and substandard medicine is invisible to packaging inspection: only a laboratory test reveals it, and the public trace of that test is the regulator's NSQ list. And roughly 3,000 NSQ batches a year is a small table, small enough to hold offline on a laptop or phone, yet large enough that an OCR misread of one batch number can collide with another product's listed batch.

### 1.3 Diethylene glycol deaths show harm arriving before any list

Four WHO Medical Product Alerts between 2022 and 2025 concerned oral liquids contaminated with diethylene glycol (DEG) or ethylene glycol. Alert N°6/2022 named four paediatric syrups from Maiden Pharmaceuticals (Haryana) identified in The Gambia ([who2022alert6](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-10-2022-medical-product-alert-n-6-2022-substandard-%28contaminated%29-paediatric-medicines)), where a parliamentary committee later attributed **at least 70 child deaths** to acute kidney injury ([reuters2022gambia](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/gambia-lawmakers-say-india-based-drug-maker-maiden-to-blame-for-child-kidney-injury-deaths-462921)). Alert N°1/2023 named two syrups from Marion Biotech (Uttar Pradesh) found in Uzbekistan and later Cambodia ([who2023alert1](https://www.who.int/news/item/11-01-2023-medical-product-alert-n-1-2023-substandard-%28contaminated%29-liquid-dosage-medicines)), linked in news reporting to 18 child deaths ([banerji2023marion](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/who-alert-on-sub-standard-cough-syrups-made-by-noida-firm-87086)). Alert N°5/2023 described NATURCOLD syrup in Cameroon with **DEG up to 28.6% against a limit of 0.10%**, labelled with a UK manufacturer that the MHRA confirmed does not exist ([who2023alert5](https://www.who.int/news/item/19-07-2023-medical-product-alert-n-5-2023--substandard-%28contaminated%29-syrup-medicines)). Alert N°5/2025 named specific batches of COLDRIF, Respifresh TR and ReLife after clusters of child deaths in India ([who2025alert5](https://www.who.int/news/item/13-10-2025-medical-product-alert-n-5-2025--substandard-%28contaminated%29-oral-liquid-medicines)); the official toll in Madhya Pradesh reached **24 children by 15 October 2025** ([tribune2025coldriftoll](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/madhya-pradesh/mp-coldrif-cough-syrup-fatality-count-reaches-24-after-2-more-children-die)), and Tamil Nadu regulators found 46-48% DEG in the implicated syrup ([tribune2025sresanarrest](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/owner-of-pharma-firm-behind-toxic-coldrif-cough-syrup-arrested-in-chennai/amp)).

The regulatory responses were all supply-side: certificates of analysis for cough-syrup exports from 1 June 2023 ([outlook2023coughsyrupexport](https://outlookindia.com/national/cough-syrups-to-undergo-testing-before-export-from-june-1-news-288654)), a revised Schedule M for good manufacturing practice notified on 28 December 2023 with smaller firms given until 31 December 2025 ([pib2025schedulem](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2102291)), and a CDSCO letter of 7 October 2025 reporting that inspected manufacturers were "not testing each batch" of excipients and active ingredients ([thakur2025coldrifletter](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/cough-syrup-deaths-drug-regulator-flags-lapses-in-testing-raw-material)). In 2026 the reported incidents were counterfeit-factory raids rather than poisonings, including a unit near Patna producing spurious zinc and azithromycin tablets ([akshitha2026patna](https://www.tmv.in/article/delhi-police-bust-fake-drug-factory-near-patna-nine-arrested-drugs-worth-5-crore-seized-date=2026-02-24)) and a Dehradun factory making fakes under the names of Cipla, Lupin, Sun Pharma and nine other major brands ([etvbharat2026dehradun](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/fake-medicines-worth-rs-5-7-crore-seized-from-dehradun-factory-enn26072302950)). Two lessons carry over to a consumer tool. In every DEG episode the deaths came before the alert, so a "not on any list" result can never be worded as "safe". And counterfeiters copy the best-known brands, which are precisely the brands under India's QR-code mandate (Section 2.1), so a decoded code or a familiar brand name is weak evidence of authenticity. NATURCOLD, contaminated and labelled with a non-existent maker, is the archetype of the identity-mismatch case that should send a tool to abstention or a SUSPECTED_FAKE review rather than to a reassuring NOT_LISTED.

### 1.4 Three monthly lists, a searchable portal since August 2025, and recalls that stop at the retailer

CDSCO publishes **three parallel monthly lists**: NSQ results from central laboratories, NSQ results from state laboratories, and a "List of spurious Drugs", alongside occasional ad hoc and revised alerts ([cdsco2026latestalerts](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Latest-Alerts)). Release dates on the Latest Alerts page expose the lag: the June 2025 lists were released on 18 July 2025 and the May 2025 spurious list on 20 June 2025, and the same page carries a "revised" May 2025 drug alert, so published rows can change after release ([cdsco2026latestalerts](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Latest-Alerts)). A CDSCO notice dated **28 August 2025** moved NSQ alerts to a dedicated link on which the "name of product, manufacturing details, NSQ results etc., are easily searchable" ([cdsco2025nsqlink](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadAlertsFiles/aret%20all%20Stakeholders.pdf)). Monthly volumes in 2025-26 ran from about 100 to 280 NSQ entries: 47 central plus 56 state entries in February 2025 ([pti2025feb](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/03/28/del114-health-drugs-quality.html)), 211 in October 2025 ([ians2025oct](https://ianslive.in/cdsco-labs-flag-211-drug-samples-as-not-of-standard-quality-in-october--20251121110525)), 280 in July 2026, when state laboratories contributed 240 entries ([etvbharat2026july](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/cdsco-flags-280-drug-samples-as-nsq-or-spurious-in-july-enn26082107816)), and 220 in August 2026, published about three weeks after month end ([pioneer2026august](https://dailypioneer.com/news/220-drug-samples-fail-quality-tests-august)). Spurious entries are rarer, between one and five a month in these reports ([pti2025apr](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/05/20/des100-health-drugs-quality.html); [ians2025oct](https://ianslive.in/cdsco-labs-flag-211-drug-samples-as-not-of-standard-quality-in-october--20251121110525)), and are usually described as an unauthorised maker using another company's brand name ([dinamalar2025sep](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/kalvimalar-news-en/central-drugs-laboratories-find-52-drug-samples-34not-of-standard-quality34-in-september-alert/57724); [etvbharat2026july](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/cdsco-flags-280-drug-samples-as-nsq-or-spurious-in-july-enn26082107816)). Officials stress that each failure applies only to the batch tested ([pioneer2026august](https://dailypioneer.com/news/220-drug-samples-fail-quality-tests-august)), and the state-laboratory table for August 2026 gives, per row, product, batch, manufacturing and expiry dates, manufacturer, failure result, reporting laboratory and reporting month, but no test date ([medicaldialogues2026august](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/cdsco/state-drug-alert-azithromycin-cefixime-among-191-drug-samples-flagged-by-cdsco-for-quality-failures-in-august-180004)). The list is therefore keyed exactly where a consumer check needs it, on product plus batch with corroborating fields, but it is a disclosure that someone has to go and read, not a notice sent to whoever holds the pack.

The recall rules make that gap explicit. CDSCO's Guidelines on Recall and Rapid Alert System, effective 23 November 2012 and reissued as Version 2024 effective 18 July 2024, classify recalls as Class I (reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death), Class II or Class III, map the Drugs Consultative Committee's NSQ categories A, B and C onto them, and require recall within **24-72 hours for Class I, 10 days for Class II and 30 days for Class III** ([cdsco2012recall](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/biologicals/4GuidelineRecalRapidAlert.pdf); [cdsco2024recall](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/biologicals/Guidelinerapid24.pdf)). The 2012 text itself concedes that "auditing and accountability is not in place" for recalls ([cdsco2012recall](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/biologicals/4GuidelineRecalRapidAlert.pdf)). Only Class I recalls reach the consumer level, through public announcements in print and electronic media; Class II recalls stop at wholesale and retail and Class III at wholesale. The 2024 SOP states that "it is preferred that information's are given by SMS or through website to the public", but only as an aspiration for Class I, with no mechanism or metric ([cdsco2024recall](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/biologicals/Guidelinerapid24.pdf)). In practice, units of a Class II or III batch already sitting in a household are never actively reached, and nobody tells a patient that a strip bought weeks earlier has since been listed. A systematic review of recall databases across 23 countries also calls for harmonised, standardised recall notifications and reports that **India was a common source of SF medicines recalled in Zambia, Sri Lanka, Brazil and Nepal** ([fryze2025substandard](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2025-103672)).

### 1.5 The demand side is the least-studied link

A 2025 scoping review of 78 studies found that knowledge of SF risks, the ability to identify SF medicines and reporting to regulators are "substantially low" among providers and the public, that harm estimates rest mostly on statistical models, and that interventions are overwhelmingly supply-side, with "a noticeable deficiency" of consumer-focused ones; it recommends digital health interventions ([pal2025assessing](https://doi.org/10.1080/20523211.2025.2550369)). A 2023 survey in Jordan puts numbers on the problem: only **24% of the public** were familiar with SF medicines, only 8% of those could identify one correctly, and 81% of members of the public who encountered one did not report it ([falah2025prevalence](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2024-018189)). The research behind this survey found **no peer-reviewed study of Indian consumers' or pharmacists' awareness of CDSCO NSQ alerts**, and no published measurement of the lag from manufacture to sampling to listing for Indian NSQ batches (Section 4.3). An ethnographic counterpoint belongs in any discussion section: in Indian pharmaceutical trade, quality and fakeness are often produced in a drug's paperwork rather than its chemistry ([thakur2023substandard](https://doi.org/10.17157/mat.10.3.7279)). A label-reading tool checks documentary identity against the regulator's documentary record. It can establish that the regulator has already tested and failed a batch; it can never establish that an unlisted batch is sound.

**Position for StripCheck.** The burden literature justifies a regulator-list lookup over visual counterfeit detection, because most failures are substandard and only laboratory testing finds them. The Indian legal framework justifies separate NSQ and spurious verdicts with different evidence thresholds. And the recall rules leave the household holder of a Class II or III batch outside every notification channel, which is the population StripCheck serves.

## 2. Existing verification answers neighbouring questions, not this one

StripCheck's question has four parts: is this *exact pack's batch* (identity at batch level) on *a regulator's list of failed samples* (a laboratory quality signal independent of the manufacturer), *read from a photo* (no special hardware or typing), and *reliably* (with misreads controlled)? Four families of existing work each answer some of these parts.

### 2.1 Codes, serials and ledgers establish provenance, not quality

India's main consumer-facing mechanism is the bar or QR code required by G.S.R. 823(E) of 17 November 2022 on the **300 brands listed in Schedule H2**, in force for batches made on or after 1 August 2023. The code must carry a unique product identification code, generic and brand names, manufacturer name and address, batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates and manufacturing licence number. The identification code "is to be decided by the manufacturer as per his own Standard Operating Procedure", coding is voluntary for other brands, and the FAQ describes no central verification database ([cdsco2023faqqr](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Final%20FAQs%20on%20QR%20code%2021.07.2023.pdf)). Bulk drugs (APIs) must carry QR codes from 1 January 2023 under G.S.R. 20(E), and CDSCO's consumer guidance reduces verification to three steps: look for the code, scan it with a mobile phone, and read the information returned "for product authenticity/genuineness" ([cdsco2025spuriousguidance](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/Guidance-for-Identification-and-Verification-of-Spurious-Drugs.pdf)). A June 2026 amendment, reported as G.S.R. 506(E), extends coding to all vaccines, antimicrobials, anticancer and NDPS-Act drugs from 1 July 2027, antimicrobials from 1 July 2028 ([roy2026qrexpansion](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/industry/pharma/health-ministry-brings-vaccines-antibiotics-cancer-drugs-under-qr-code-tracking-framework-173735)), and the public broadcaster presents it as protection against "counterfeit and substandard medicines" ([newsonair2026qrexpansion](https://newsonair.gov.in/health-ministry-expands-qr-code-based-track-and-trace-framework-for-medicines/)). That framing conflates provenance with quality: a correctly coded genuine batch can still fail assay. Unit-level, GS1-style serialization with parent-child aggregation exists in India only for exports, through the iVEDA portal that replaced DAVA in 2020 ([pharmexcil2020iveda](https://pharmexcil.com/circulars/viewcirculars/10445/e38beefbf779b717e688f7b78f8f5768.html)), with the aggregation deadline extended to 1 February 2025 ([dgft2024pn39](https://taxguru.in/dgft/dgft-extends-track-trace-deadline-drug-exports.html)). The weakness of scan-to-trust surfaced in September 2026, when Karnataka regulators reported about Rs 5.05 crore of suspected counterfeits whose QR codes "did not function when verified", and consumers were told to report codes that fail to scan ([rao2026karnataka](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/karnataka-suspends-pfizer-cf-depot-licence-fake-drug-case-warns-consumers-over-suspicious-labels-906210)). A copied code, by contrast, scans perfectly.

Western systems are stronger but serve the trade, not the patient. The EU Falsified Medicines Directive (2011/62/EU) requires a unique identifier and an anti-tampering device on the outer pack, with verification rules set by Delegated Regulation 2016/161, applicable since 9 February 2019 ([ec2026falsifiedmedicines](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/falsified-medicines_en)); WHO classes the EU model as point-of-dispense verification ([who2021traceability](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/policy-paper-on-traceability-of-medical-products)). A systematic review of its effects on stakeholders found only 13 studies, mostly on cost and workflow burden, concluded that the regime "does not yet appear to categorically fulfill" its aim, and cites a 2023 German case in which falsified semaglutide was caught because the scanned serial numbers were inactive ([melia2024impact](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2024.1429872)). The US Drug Supply Chain Security Act's enhanced interoperable tracing, due on 27 November 2023, has been phased in through successive exemptions ([trujillo2024dscsa](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/fda-issues-broad-exemptions-from-dscsa-enhanced-drug-distribution-security-requirements)), with small dispensers now exempt until 27 November 2027 ([fda2026dscsaexemptions](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-supply-chain-security-act-dscsa/exemptions-under-drug-supply-chain-security-act)). GS1 identifiers underpin both regimes: Application Identifier (10) carries the batch or lot as free alphanumeric text of up to 20 characters alongside the GTIN (01), production date (11), expiry (17) and serial (21) ([gs12026ai](https://ref.gs1.org/ai/)), so even a machine-read batch must be normalised before it can be joined to a hand-typed regulator table. Klein and Stolk draw the distinction that matters here: the same code serves authentication against falsification and batch-level traceability, and only the second, which can "accelerate batch recalls", addresses quality problems in genuine product ([klein2018traceability](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-018-0678-7)).

Mobile authentication services and blockchain sit further still from the quality question. SMS scratch-code services such as mPedigree (from 2006), Sproxil (from 2009) and PharmaSecure, which also operated in India, reply whether a manufacturer-issued code is "tagged genuine or fake" ([rasheed2018future](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2018.00995)). In Nigeria only 53% of 326 community pharmacists were keen to use the national service, and providers blamed missing or wrong replies on GSM downtime and power cuts, failures that made some legitimate pharmacies look like sources of fakes ([oyetunde2019mobile](https://doi.org/10.18549/pharmpract.2019.2.1449)). Blockchain traceability remained mostly prototypes and simulations in a 38-paper systematic review ([zakari2022blockchain](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.840)); in FDA's DSCSA pilot programme the MediLedger pilot set design targets of more than 2,000 transactions per second and sub-second verification but reported only a test user interface, and FDA's lessons concerned standards, master-data quality and governance rather than ledger technology ([fda2023dscsapilot](https://www.fda.gov/media/168307/download)).

The decisive point is stated plainly by WHO: verification techniques "cannot check the authenticity of the chemicals, the drug or the active pharmaceutical ingredient inside the package"; they confirm only that genuine identifiers were used. WHO also calls patient-side verification a "last resort" and records that rogue actors have replicated verification services that answer every query positively ([who2021traceability](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/policy-paper-on-traceability-of-medical-products)). Bansal et al. make the same split from an Indian industry perspective, authentication technologies "verify the supply chain" while analytical methods "confirm the composition", and warn that overt security features can be re-used or refilled and "provide false assurance" ([bansal2013anticounterfeit](https://doi.org/10.3797/scipharm.1202-03)); a 2026 trade-press analysis adds that serialization "does not and cannot authenticate the physical product" ([ohearen2026serialized](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/the-dark-side-serialized-but-not-secure)). Because WHO defines substandard products as *authorised* products that fail specification ([who2017definitions](https://www.who.int/teams/regulation-prequalification/incidents-and-SF/background/definitions)), **a genuine NSQ batch passes every code check**. Mackey and Nayyar's taxonomy of digital anti-counterfeiting technologies (mobile, RFID, advanced computational methods, online verification and blockchain) ([mackey2017review](https://doi.org/10.1080/14740338.2017.1313227)) has no slot for checking a pack against the regulator's own test results, which is where StripCheck sits.

### 2.2 Field screening tests chemistry, but needs devices, training and the right reference library

Portable chemical screening attacks quality directly, at a price. Kovacs et al. reviewed 42 technologies and found costs from under US$1 per test for paper cards to about US$3,780 for a GPHF-Minilab, about US$50,000 for Raman or NIR spectrometers and US$350,000-375,000 for GC-MS or HPLC-MS; the Minilab flags only grossly substandard products (below 80% of stated API), "no single technology" serves all needs, and "technologies alone will not solve the problem" ([kovacs2014technologies](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0090601)). Vickers et al. found 41 portable devices in 62 studies but only six evaluated in the field, with a median of two APIs tested per device; one device's field specificity was only 53-64%, the Minilab detected 30% of very non-compliant antimalarial samples in a multi-country survey, and the authors called the lack of independent field evaluation "vitally important" ([vickers2018field](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2018-000725)). The most instructive result comes from a comparative field trial of six devices with 16 medicine inspectors in Laos. Misclassification ranged from 0% (TruScan RM, MicroPHAZIR RX) to 37.9% (paper analytical devices), device costs from US$3 to US$62,500, and inspectors chose the **wrong reference library in 27.0% and 20.0% of scans** on two spectrometers, and most of the TruScan library errors went unrecognised; the authors warn that "overconfidence in devices may cause harm" ([caillet2021comparative](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0009674)). A silent wrong lookup that still returns a confident answer is exactly the failure an OCR misread causes in a batch lookup, which is why StripCheck treats verification of the read, not just the lookup, as a first-class step.

WHO's 2025 landscape report frames SF detection as a task in which regulators, health ministries and other stakeholders play "equally crucial roles" ([who2025report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240103559)). Its June 2026 draft guidance on selecting screening technologies covers Raman, NIR, MIR, TLC/HPTLC and paper devices, with digital elements limited to aids for those tests, such as smartphone documentation of TLC plates and an app that reads paper-device results ([who2026guidance](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/health-products-policy-and-standards/qas26_1004guidancescreeningtechnologies.pdf); [eca2026whodraft](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/who-draft-guidance-selecting-the-right-analytical-screening-technologies-for-substandard-and-falsified-medicines)). Information-based screening, checking a pack against published test results, lies outside the guidance's scope.

### 2.3 Consumer apps identify pills and judge appearance, not batch quality

A 2021 systematic review of 25 apps and 16 websites for detecting SF medicines found that most identify pills by imprint, colour and shape, that a single accuracy study existed (MedSnap, 48 samples, rated poor quality with high risk of bias), and that mean app quality on the Mobile App Rating Scale was 3.17 of 5; the authors describe "a remarkable evidence gap" about accuracy ([ciapponi2021mobile](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0246061)). Recognising the object from a consumer photo is itself hard: in the NLM pill image recognition challenge the winning mean average precision was 0.27, with the correct pill in the top five for only 43% of 5,000 consumer-quality queries ([yaniv2016national](https://doi.org/10.1109/AIPR.2016.8010584)), although small-footprint mobile pill recognisers have been built ([zeng2017mobiledeeppill](https://doi.org/10.1145/3081333.3081336)). Appearance-based counterfeit detection performs modestly and depends on references. GPT-4o, prompted under three image configurations (query only, with a genuine reference image, and with a difference image), reached at most **74.6% accuracy** in classifying authentic versus counterfeit packaging ([zakaria2025counterfeit](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3071640)); a smartphone CNN reached 99.71% accuracy only after rejecting 3.06% of inputs, and was tested on branded garments rather than medicines ([garciacotte2024deep](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05969)); and a Nigerian app combining YOLO-NAS logo detection (mAP 79.3%) with cloud OCR was evaluated for usability with 16 students rather than for counterfeit-detection accuracy ([edegbe2025development](https://doi.org/10.11113/ijic.v15n1.486)). India's national consumer-scanning app, GS1 India's Smart Consumer, displays product details such as name, manufacturing date, MRP and manufacturer, with no mention of recalls or quality tests ([gs1india2024smart](https://gs1india.org/blog/smart-consumer-app)). None of these tools consults a regulator's test results, and none could detect a genuine-looking substandard batch, which is the majority case (Section 1.2). The rejection rate in the garment study is itself a lesson: high precision in counterfeit vision is bought with an abstain option.

### 2.4 Seven projects reused CDSCO NSQ data in 2025-2026, and the closest already reads photos

The move to a searchable NSQ portal and public JSON (Section 1.4) has produced a small ecosystem of open projects, documented here from their READMEs and pages as accessed on 25 October 2026 (code not audited). **Blacklisted** offers a typed-batch lookup over 6,626 failed medicine batches from CDSCO and state laboratories since January 2019, plus 683 banned medicines, with a monthly email digest and RSS feed ([blacklisted2026](https://blacklisted.co.in)). **Nazar**, whose README describes it as live as of 18 October 2026, checks batches against CDSCO, state and WHO alerts from typed input, on-device QR/Data Matrix/GS1 decoding and photo extraction of the printed batch line. It returns four states (Flagged, Retracted, No alert found, Can't tell), never shows "No alert found" as green, and applies a look-alike guard for OCR confusions (0/O, 1/I/L, 5/S, 8/B) that returns Can't tell with a "Did you mean...?" suggestion rather than a flag; its ingest is deterministic first, with LLM extraction only for leftovers under a rule that every extracted value must appear in the source text ([rastogi2026nazar](https://github.com/pranshurastogi/Nazar)). **SafeDose** checks a typed drug name and batch against one month of alerts (70 flagged batches) and returns "caution" when the batch matches under a different drug name ([shaurya2026safedose](https://github.com/shaurya703/safedose)). **RecallOps India**, a distributor-side hackathon agent, places a stock hold only when batch and product match and at least one of manufacturer or dates corroborates without contradiction, routes uncertain rows to human review, never lets an unsure LLM answer create a hold, reopens decisions when a new alert arrives, and offers a deterministic no-LLM mode; on the author's synthetic fixtures it made 61/61 and 92/93 correct holds with no wrong holds, against 56/61 and 80/93 for name-only matching ([ibnweb32026recallops](https://github.com/ibnweb3/recallops-india)). Three further projects are analytics or ingest pipelines: one resolves manufacturer-name variants with fuzzy matching and union-find and drops parsed rows whose batch and drug name do not appear on the source page ([swahub2026nsq](https://github.com/Swa-hub/nsq-medicine-tracker)), one builds dashboards on CDSCO's public NSQ JSON table ([shayadmeri2026nsq](https://github.com/shayadmeri-svg/nsq)), and one is a cloud scraping pipeline ([shaikfayaz2026medicine](https://github.com/ShaikFayaz042/medicine-scanner-research)).

A September 2026 case shows why corroboration needs a third branch beyond "match" and "collision". A Telma AM batch in the August 2026 alert was listed with manufacturer "Under Investigation" after the labelled maker denied making it, with CDSCO remarking that "the product is purported to be spurious. However, the same is subject to outcome of investigation" ([roy2026telma](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/cdsco/cdsco-flags-telma-am-batch-as-purportedly-spurious-manufacturer-denies-making-it-179924)). On a spurious entry, a mismatch between the printed maker and the regulator's record is evidence of fakery, not a collision to discard, and the entry itself is provisional.

### 2.5 The unanswered question, and what remains novel

Read against the four-part question, each family covers a different slice. Codes and ledgers establish batch identity for coded packs but carry no quality signal and are defeated by copying. Chemical screening produces a quality signal but needs a device, training and the correct reference library, and is not aimed at consumers. Consumer apps read photos but answer identity or appearance questions. The NSQ tools of 2025-2026 bring the regulator's laboratory signal to typed batch numbers, and Nazar adds photo reading and a look-alike guard. **No tool surveyed combines photographed-pack input, confusion-aware batch search, field-by-field corroboration that separates collisions from spurious-pattern mismatches, a human question when a character is in doubt, a verifier that can abstain, and fully offline operation**, and none reports an evaluation on real photographed packs. Because every individual feature has a precedent, StripCheck's defensible claim is an integration and safety-policy contribution backed by an empirical evaluation, hedged as "among the tools found as of October 2026"; GitHub projects change quickly, so access dates belong in the citations.

## 3. Every component technique fails confidently unless something outside it checks the output

StripCheck chains six techniques, each with its own literature: reading the pack (3.1); turning a noisy read into candidate batch strings and matching them against a hotlist (3.2); deciding whether a listed row and a photographed pack are the same record (3.3); orchestrating these steps with an agent whose output an external verifier checks (3.4); abstaining and wording the verdict (3.5); and building the list itself from regulator documents (3.6). The thread running through all six literatures is the same: each step produces fluent, confident errors, and reliability comes from independent checks placed around the step rather than from trusting its own score.

### 3.1 Packaging OCR reads dates well and batch codes poorly, and its confidence is not correctness

**Dates are a solved-looking niche; batch codes are not.** Expiry-date recognition on consumer packages reaches about 97% on curated data: Seker and Ahn report 97.74% recognition across 13 date formats, with a neural date parser and a public ExpDate dataset released because none existed ([seker2022generalized](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2022.117310)), and Peng et al. report detection F1 of 0.9303 and 97.06% recognition in 63 ms on one GPU, with FP16 on CPU the best accuracy-energy trade-off for mobile ([peng2025efficient](https://doi.org/10.3390/a18050286)). Dot-matrix printing needs dedicated handling: Zidane and Soliman translate dotted Arabic dates into filled glyphs before recognition, trained on 60,000 synthetic images because real dotted-date data were unavailable ([zidane2024ladder](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JEI.33.5.053024)), and Kuri et al. combine OCR of expiry text with CNN and GNN features to reach 0.91 accuracy in detecting expired medicines ([kuri2025noninvasive](https://doi.org/10.1051/bioconf/202520401021)). Batch codes on medicine packs are almost unstudied. The one pharmaceutical paper found, Koponen et al., reads dot-matrix and ink-marked expiry dates and batch codes with an R-CNN at **precision 91.1%, recall 72.7% and F1 80.9%, against Tesseract's 38.3%, 61.3% and 47.1%**, but on only 27 binarised images from one company, with mixed peer review, and it explicitly excludes un-inked embossed, stamped and laser-printed text ([koponen2024novel](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.131775.2)). This survey found no peer-reviewed benchmark of OCR on embossed or foil-printed blister batch codes and no public dataset of Indian strips with annotated batch and date fields. Accessibility apps show the same product-not-batch focus: a Korean app for visually impaired users recognises containers, pills and prescriptions ([kim2023implementation](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-99-3243-6_73)), and Microsoft Seeing AI reads product information for more than 1,500 Haleon products by barcode, prompted by a survey in which 93% of 502 visually impaired respondents said health products were not accessible enough and almost one in five had taken a wrong dose because they could not read the packaging ([pharmacymagazine2022newai](https://www.pharmacymagazine.co.uk/news/882582-new-ai-helps-visually-impaired)). Dates have a grammar that a parser can validate (formats, manufacture before expiry, plausible ranges); batch codes have none, so a batch read can only be corroborated from outside, by the other fields of the regulator's row.

**The lightweight OCR stack is PP-OCR.** The PP-OCR line chains a DB text detector, a direction classifier and a CTC recogniser, with a 2.8 MB model for 63 alphanumeric symbols ([du2020ppocr](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2009.09941)), improved in PP-OCRv2 ([du2021ppocrv2](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.03144)) and PP-OCRv3, whose SVTR-LCNet recogniser raised hmean by 5% at comparable speed ([li2022ppocrv3](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2206.03001)). PaddleOCR 3.0 introduced PP-OCRv5 and claims that models under 100M parameters rival billion-parameter VLMs, ranking first on average on a vendor-run 17-scenario benchmark ahead of GOT-OCR2.0, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-4o ([cui2025paddleocr](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.05595)); this should be cited as the vendor's claim. RapidOCR packages these models as Apache-2.0 ONNX for offline CPU inference ([rapidai2021rapidocr](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR)), which is what makes a fully offline reader on a Windows laptop practical. The scene-text surveys ([long2021scene](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-020-01369-0); [chen2021text](https://doi.org/10.1145/3440756)) frame packaging text correctly, as text on arbitrary backgrounds under perspective and uneven lighting rather than as documents. The choice of decoder matters for lot numbers. TrOCR's autoregressive Transformer decoder generates wordpieces with no separate language-model post-processing ([li2023trocr](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v37i11.26538)), so its implicit language prior can pull an arbitrary code towards plausible text; a CTC recogniser has no lexical prior, which avoids that "autocorrection" but leaves visually similar glyphs (B/8, O/0, S/5, 1/7/I) to be confused on appearance alone. That confusion set is exactly what StripCheck's variant search targets (Section 3.2).

**Vision-language models read documents fluently and invent characters on degraded inputs.** Generative readers multiplied in 2024-2025: GOT, a 580M-parameter "OCR-2.0" model ([wei2024general](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.01704)); Qwen2.5-VL, whose 72B flagship claims parity with GPT-4o on document understanding ([bai2025qwen25vl](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.13923)); olmOCR, a 7B model costing about US$176 per million PDF pages ([poznanski2025olmocr](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.18443)); the 0.9B PaddleOCR-VL ([cui2025paddleocrvl](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.14528)); DeepSeek-OCR, whose decoding precision falls from about 97% below 10x visual-token compression to about 60% at 20x ([wei2025deepseekocr](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.18234)); and Qwen3-VL in dense sizes from 2B to 32B ([bai2025qwen3vl](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.21631)). All are optimised for document parsing, and the evidence on short codes is discouraging. On the KIE-HVQA benchmark of degraded identity cards, invoices and prescriptions, models "default to linguistic priors rather than anchoring decisions to observable visual evidence", Qwen2.5-VL-7B identifies "values not present in the image", and even the authors' trained model scores only 58.05 against GPT-4o's 30.21 ([he2025seeing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20168)). Guan et al. show that VLMs produce fluent but incorrect text on lossy inputs without signalling uncertainty, and that training them to tag unreliable spans reaches an uncertainty-tag F1 of only 0.685 ([guan2026teaching](https://openreview.net/forum?id=zyCjizqOxB)). OCRBench v2 finds most large multimodal models below 50 of 100, with named weaknesses in "less frequently encountered text recognition" and "fine-grained perception" ([fu2024ocrbenchv2](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.00321)), and a batch code is by construction infrequent, non-lexical text. A VLM can therefore serve only as an independent second reader whose disagreement with the primary OCR triggers a question or abstention, never as the sole source of a batch number; a reader from a different model family is the better second opinion, because shared training lineage weakens error independence.

**A high score does not certify a correct read.** Modern neural networks are poorly calibrated, and temperature scaling is a surprisingly effective post-hoc fix ([guo2017calibration](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)). Calibration work on scene-text recognisers ([slossberg2022calibration](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-25069-9_18)) and on sequence recognisers, which are "prone to over-confidence" when trained on target sequences alone ([peng2023perception](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52729.2023.01027)), extends the point to OCR, and Yao et al. show that a VLM's own output probabilities are a poor gate for OCR abstention, using a "50 mph" read as "60 mph" to motivate probes on hidden states that improve abstention accuracy by 7.6% ([yao2025reading](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.19806)). Image enhancement does not rescue a misread either. CLAHE ([pizer1987adaptive](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0734-189X%2887%2980186-X); [zuiderveld1994contrast](https://doi.org/10.1016/b978-0-12-336156-1.50061-6)) redistributes local contrast, and the only controlled study on short alphanumeric codes found, on licence plates, measured 70.75% accuracy with CLAHE against a 71.7% baseline ([tavares2024comparison](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.13622)). StripCheck's enhanced re-read is therefore best framed as a second hypothesis under different contrast, to be reconciled with the first, not as a correction.

**Position for StripCheck.** The literature supports QR decoding first (exact bits when a code exists), PP-OCR via RapidOCR as the primary offline reader, an enhanced re-read and an optional local VLM as independent hypotheses, and no verdict gated on raw OCR confidence. It also leaves two cheap, publishable contributions open: a reliability diagram or expected calibration error for RapidOCR scores on real strip photos, and the first annotated set of Indian strip batch and date fields.

### 3.2 A batch lookup is a noisy-channel decoder over a closed hotlist, and ALPR shows what goes wrong

**The noisy-channel formulation fits exactly.** Kernighan, Church and Gale's spelling corrector chooses the candidate *c* that maximises Pr(*c*)Pr(*t*|*c*) for a typed string *t*, with channel probabilities computed from four confusion matrices for deletion, insertion, substitution and reversal ([kernighan1990spelling](https://doi.org/10.3115/997939.997975)). Wagner and Fischer's dynamic programme finds the minimum-cost edit sequence between two strings in time proportional to the product of their lengths ([wagner1974string](https://doi.org/10.1145/321796.321811)); with substitution costs taken from an OCR confusion matrix (cheap for B/8 or O/0, expensive otherwise) it becomes a confusion-aware matcher, and batch strings are short enough to score exhaustively against a pre-filtered candidate set. Brill and Moore's improved error model, which learns multi-character edits from error pairs, reached 95.0% top-1 accuracy against a 200,000-entry dictionary, against 89.5% for the earlier weighted-edit model, a 52% relative error reduction; even with single-character edits only, accuracy rose from 88.7% at top-1 to 95.1% at top-2 and 96.6% at top-3 ([brill2000improved](https://doi.org/10.3115/1075218.1075255)). The right answer is usually among the first few candidates, which argues for passing a short ranked list to corroboration and to the user rather than auto-selecting one string. Kolak, Byrne and Resnik model OCR generatively, treat the engine as a black box and decode its output into lexicon words with weighted finite-state transducers under an explicit closed-vocabulary assumption, cutting word error rate by 70% to reach 97% word accuracy on French text read by an English OCR engine ([kolak2003generative](https://doi.org/10.3115/1073445.1073463); see also [kolak2002ocr](https://doi.org/10.3115/1289189.1289208)); a follow-up reports relative error reductions as high as 78% for Igbo ([kolak2005ocr](https://doi.org/10.3115/1220575.1220684)). In StripCheck the CDSCO batch index plays the dictionary's role, as a far smaller and more structured lexicon. That raises precision but creates a **collision risk**, in which a misread genuine batch lands exactly on a different listed batch, and it is why field corroboration (Section 3.3) has to play the part that the language model plays in the classic formulation.

**Detecting an error is easier than correcting it, and LLM correctors over-correct.** Post-OCR processing has a reference survey ([nguyen2021survey](https://doi.org/10.1145/3453476)) and two ICDAR competitions ([chiron2017icdar](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2017.232); [rigaud2019icdar](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2019.00255)). In the 2019 round, over 22 million OCR'd symbols in ten European languages, error-detection scores reached 95% while the best correction improvement was 44% ([rigaud2019icdar](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2019.00255)). LLM correctors help on long prose with context: the best of seven language models cut character error rate by more than 60% on one historical newspaper corpus, but only "some LMs" helped significantly and misleading prompts hurt ([bourne2024clocrc](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2408.17428)); open-weight LLMs reduced error for historical English, but "a practically useful performance for Finnish was not reached" ([kanerva2025ocr](https://aclanthology.org/2025.resourceful-1.8/); see also [thomas2024leveraging](https://doi.org/10.63317/5aezhy5iacm9)). The ICDAR 2026 HIPE-OCRepair competition names hallucination as a concern and identifies **over-correction of low-noise inputs** as a recurring failure ([ehrmann2026hipe](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-36042-7_24)). For a batch number, an LLM that "fixes" a correct read into a different plausible string is the worst possible failure, because it can turn NOT_LISTED into a false FLAGGED or hide a listed batch. Every one of these benchmarks uses running text; none evaluates correction of short, context-free alphanumeric identifiers, which is the regime in which language priors help least and risk most.

**ALPR hotlist matching is the closest deployed analogue, and its failures are documented.** The standard ALPR pipeline of plate localisation, character segmentation and recognition is surveyed by Du et al. ([du2013alpr](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSVT.2012.2203741)). Laroca et al.'s layout-independent YOLO system reaches 96.9% end-to-end recognition across eight public datasets, stresses that "a single mistake" can misidentify a vehicle, and publishes explicit swap tables for confusable characters (letters to digits A→4, B→8, D→0, G→6, I→1, J→1, Q→0, S→5, Z→7; digits to letters 1→I, 2→Z, 4→A, 5→S, 6→G, 7→Z, 8→B), applied only where the plate layout fixes which positions hold letters or digits ([laroca2021efficient](https://doi.org/10.1049/itr2.12030); see also [laroca2018robust](https://doi.org/10.1109/IJCNN.2018.8489629)). Indian batch numbers follow no national format, so StripCheck cannot hard-swap a character; it has to generate both alternatives and let the closed index and corroboration decide. Seattle's audit of police ALPR counted 976 and 948 logged misreads against 5,691 and 4,683 hits in 2021 and 2022, **about one misread for every five unverified hits**, which the auditor calls an undercount; its examples include "1" read as "I", "8" read as "B" and identical characters on plates from different states. Policy requires officers to compare the image with the hotlist record for state and characters, confirm by dispatch before acting and record an Accept or Misread disposition for every hit; two further problem incidents stemmed from outdated or misleading hotlist data, one of them a high-risk stop resolved only by a VIN check ([seattleoig2023alpr](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/departments/oig/audits/surveillancetechnologyusagereview-automatedlicenseplatereader-patrol%282021and2022%29.pdf)). In *Green v. City and County of San Francisco*, plate 5SOW350 was read as 5SOW750 and matched a stolen grey GMC truck although Green was driving a burgundy Lexus; the night image was illegible, the sergeant compared only the first three characters, and Green was held at gunpoint and handcuffed. The record treats as undisputed that the ALPR "occasionally makes false 'hits'", the opinion quotes the IACP model policy requiring visual verification and a status check prior to initiating a stop, and it notes that the make, model and colour mismatch arguably justified further investigation ([green2014ninth](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/05/12/11-17892.pdf)). Civil-liberties commentary draws the general lesson that machine identifications turn innocent people into suspects when treated as conclusive ([lynch2014ninth](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2014/05/new-ninth-circuit-opinion-calls-question-blind-reliance-license-plate-camera-ids); [aclu2013tracked](https://www.aclu.org/documents/you-are-being-tracked-how-license-plate-readers-are-being-used-record-americans-movements)). The Green facts map one-to-one onto StripCheck's risks: a one-character confusion against a closed list, an illegible image, a partial human check and an ignored field mismatch. The countermeasures are a confusion-aware search that returns ranked candidates, CANT_VERIFY on illegible images, a per-character question to the user, field corroboration that rejects collisions, and a date-stamped list version on every verdict.

**Human answers on doubtful characters can train the channel model.** reCAPTCHA sent humans only "suspicious" words, where two OCR engines disagreed or the output was not in a dictionary; about 96% of suspicious words were wrong in at least one engine, while 99.74% of the others were right in both. Counting each human answer as one vote, each OCR guess as half a vote and requiring 2.5 votes, it reached **99.1% word accuracy against 83.5% for OCR alone**, matching professional double-keying ([vonahn2008recaptcha](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1160379)). Fink et al. feed users' corrections back into an OCR error profile, raising precision in detecting erroneous tokens ([fink2017profiling](https://doi.org/10.1145/3078081.3078096)), and interactive post-correction tools such as PoCoTo and OCR4all build the human into the OCR workflow ([vobl2014pocoto](https://doi.org/10.1145/2595188.2595197); [reul2019ocr4all](https://doi.org/10.3390/app9224853)). StripCheck's character confirmations are the same kind of label as Seattle's Accept/Misread disposition: logged as (read, confirmed) pairs, they can re-estimate confusion weights by smoothed counting, exactly as the classic channel models were trained from error pairs. Two questions remain open in the literature: no published confusion matrix exists for pharmaceutical batch-code printing (dot-matrix, inkjet or embossed on foil), and no study was found on how reliable lay users are when asked about a blurry character, as opposed to expert transcribers.

**Position for StripCheck.** The variant search is a textbook noisy-channel decoder over a closed lexicon, seeded with published ALPR confusion pairs; the human question on doubtful characters is reCAPTCHA's doubt-routing applied per query; and the verifier's rule that a variant match alone never yields FLAGGED is the ALPR verification policy that *Green* shows to be necessary. StripCheck's own measured confusion table for batch-code printing would be a new resource.

### 3.3 Record linkage supplies the decision theory for collisions and for the human band

**Fellegi-Sunter maps directly onto StripCheck's verdicts.** Newcombe et al. linked British Columbia birth records to 114,471 marriage records by comparing only records that shared Soundex-coded surname pairs (de facto blocking) and summing per-field log-odds weights, with agreement on rare values weighted more than agreement on common ones; they detected about 98.3% of potential linkages with 0.7% spurious ([newcombe1959automatic](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.130.3381.954)). Fellegi and Sunter formalised the idea: for a vector γ of field agreements, the optimal rule orders pairs by the likelihood ratio m(γ)/u(γ) and uses two thresholds, set from tolerated error rates, to assign each pair to link (A1), possible link (A2) or non-link (A3), minimising the possible-link region because it "will require expensive manual linkage operations" ([fellegi1969theory](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1969.10501049)). The mapping is exact. The batch number is the blocking key; product, brand, dates and maker form the comparison vector; FLAGGED and SUSPECTED_FAKE are A1, NOT_LISTED is A3, and the human question and CANT_VERIFY are A2. The theory also says the two thresholds may sit asymmetrically, because telling a user that a genuine medicine is listed and missing a listed batch carry different costs. Winkler's extension of Jaro's comparator, used in production matching for the 1990 US Census, showed that graded partial-agreement weights keep the Fellegi-Sunter rules valid ([winkler1990string](http://www.asasrms.org/Proceedings/papers/1990_056.pdf); [jaro1989advances](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1989.10478785)). His 2006 overview supplies the operational numbers: in a census application exact character matching would have missed more than 25% of true matches; EM estimation of the m and u probabilities works only when matches make up more than about 5% of the pair set; fewer than 0.5% labelled pairs drawn from the clerical-review region improve error-rate estimation; and computerised linkage cut one census's clerical review from 75 clerks working three months to 6,500 hours ([winkler2006overview](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2006/adrm/rrs2006-02.pdf)). Because a randomly photographed strip almost never matches the NSQ list, matches are far rarer than 5%, so hand-set or rule-derived weights calibrated on a small labelled set from the uncertain band are the defensible choice. Elmagarmid et al.'s survey supplies the layered architecture (field comparators, record-level decision, blocking for efficiency), notes that the Fellegi-Sunter reject class is where "a clerical review is necessary", and reports that no single similarity metric suits all datasets ([elmagarmid2007duplicate](https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2007.250581)); Christen's book is the standard textbook ([christen2012data](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-31164-2)).

**Blocking on a misread key silently loses the match.** Christen's indexing survey warns that "any error in a field value used to generate a BKV will potentially result in records being inserted into the wrong block, thus leading to missing true matches", and recommends multiple blocking keys or multiple encodings of the same key ([christen2012survey](https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2011.127)). Papadakis et al. distinguish blocking from filtering (similarity joins that return every pair within a threshold) and define pair completeness, pairs quality and reduction ratio as the metrics for candidate generation ([papadakis2020survey](https://doi.org/10.1145/3377455)). StripCheck's confusion-variant search is a domain-specific case of "multiple encodings of the same key", or equivalently a filter over a confusion-weighted edit distance, and pair completeness is the right metric for it: did the true alert row reach the candidate set at all? This survey found no published study of blocking on pharmaceutical batch or lot numbers, or of OCR-confusion-aware blocking keys.

**Clerical review can become a question to the person holding the pack.** Hybrid entity resolution keeps Fellegi and Sunter's division of labour: CrowdER uses machines for a coarse pass and sends only likely matches to crowd workers ([wang2012crowder](https://doi.org/10.14778/2350229.2350263)), and active learners such as ALIAS ask people to label only high-uncertainty pairs ([sarawagi2002interactive](https://doi.org/10.1145/775047.775087), as described in [elmagarmid2007duplicate](https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2007.250581)). StripCheck pushes clerical review down to a single field, often a single character, and puts it to the one person who can re-read the physical strip; this framing of field-level clerical review is not stated in the sources and is part of StripCheck's contribution. The Fellegi-Sunter objective then becomes a measurable design target: minimise questions per scan while holding false-FLAGGED and missed-listing rates below set limits.

**Each field needs its own comparator, and Indian brand names are unreliable keys.** Cohen et al. found that a hybrid, SoftTF-IDF with Jaro-Winkler as the secondary similarity, did best overall on multi-token names (MaxF1 0.89 against 0.73 for a Jaro-Winkler baseline on one dataset), while Jaro and Jaro-Winkler are "intended primarily for short strings" ([cohen2003comparison](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf)). For StripCheck that suggests exact or confusion-weighted edit distance for the batch (every character matters), a prefix-sensitive comparator for brands, token-based similarity after normalisation for product descriptions and manufacturer names (dropping tokens such as "IP", "Tab." and "Pvt Ltd"), and month-level tolerance for dates. RxNorm's split of a clinical drug into ingredient, strength and dose form, with the brand as a separate element, is a ready schema, but its scope is the United States ([nlm2025rxnorm](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/umls/rxnorm/overview.html)). In India brand names collide across molecules: "Medzol" is used for both midazolam and pantoprazole, "Linamac" for both lenalidomide and linagliptin, and a single molecule such as molnupiravir is sold under at least seven brands ([neelakantan2024lookalike](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2024.100425)). Brand agreement alone is therefore weak evidence and must never produce FLAGGED; generic, strength, manufacturer and batch have to agree.

**LLMs can adjudicate a small candidate set, but not replace the rules.** Narayan et al. showed that GPT-3 with ten demonstrations matched or beat the fine-tuned Ditto matcher on several benchmark datasets, but also that changing the prompt from "same?" to "equivalent?" moved F1 by 9.4 points ([narayan2022foundation](https://doi.org/10.14778/3574245.3574258)). Peeters et al. found zero-shot GPT-4 competitive with fine-tuned models (for example 89.61 F1 against Ditto's 84.90 on WDC Products) and robust to unseen entities where a fine-tuned RoBERTa lost 22-61 F1 points, but the standard deviation of F1 across prompts was 2.26 points for GPT-4 against 6.18-18.54 for other models, and explicit matching rules helped weaker open models more than GPT-4 ([peeters2025entity](https://doi.org/10.48786/edbt.2025.42)). Zhang et al. report that a fine-tuned 1.3B model matched GPT-4 in cross-dataset matching (87.5 against 87.4 average F1) at orders of magnitude lower deployment cost ([zhang2025deep](https://doi.org/10.48786/edbt.2025.75)), and Wang et al. show that asking an LLM to *select* the matching record from a candidate list beats independent pairwise yes/no decisions ([wang2025match](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.8/)).

**Position for StripCheck.** Deterministic blocking and field rules should decide most cases, with a small local LLM selecting "one candidate or none" only in the residual possible-match band, given explicit field rules in its prompt and a deterministic fallback. Published LLM matching scores come from 70B-class or hosted models on English product data, so they are an upper bound for a few-billion-parameter local model on Indian drug records; StripCheck has to report its own figures, separating candidate-generation recall from verdict accuracy.

### 3.4 Agent architectures converge on "the LLM proposes, tools and rules dispose"

**Self-correction fails without external grounding.** ReAct interleaves reasoning traces with actions that query external sources, reducing the hallucination and error propagation of pure chain-of-thought and improving absolute success by 34% on ALFWorld and 10% on WebShop from one or two examples ([yao2023react](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)). MRKL proposed a modular neuro-symbolic architecture that combines neural models with discrete knowledge and reasoning modules, because language models alone are limited in both ([karpas2022mrkl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00445)), and Toolformer showed that a model can learn when and how to call APIs ([schick2023toolformer](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-2997)). Self-feedback methods report gains, such as Reflexion's 91% pass@1 on HumanEval against 80% for the GPT-4 baseline ([shinn2023reflexion](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-0377)) and Self-Refine's roughly 20% average absolute improvement across seven tasks ([madaan2023selfrefine](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-2019)), but these rely on external test feedback or strong models. CRITIC found that verification and revision work when grounded in external tools such as search and code execution, and concluded that external feedback is crucial ([gou2024critic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738)). Huang et al. showed the converse: without external feedback, models struggle to correct their own reasoning, and accuracy sometimes falls after self-correction ([huang2024cannot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). Kambhampati et al. generalise the point: autoregressive LLMs "cannot, by themselves, do planning or self-verification", so the LLM-Modulo framework pairs an LLM that generates candidates with sound external verifiers in a critique loop ([kambhampati2024llmmodulo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01817)). This cluster is the core justification for StripCheck's verifier: the LLM may propose (which candidate row, which re-read to try), a rule-based verifier that the LLM cannot overrule accepts or rejects, and CANT_VERIFY results when no candidate survives.

**Workflows beat open-ended autonomy when the steps are known.** Anthropic's engineering guidance distinguishes workflows, in which LLMs and tools follow predefined code paths, from agents that direct their own process, and recommends the simplest solution that works, programmatic checks between steps, ground truth from the environment at each step, human checkpoints and explicit stopping conditions, because errors compound ([schluntz2024building](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). Multi-agent frameworks range from SOP-encoded role pipelines, motivated by the cascading hallucinations of naively chained LLMs ([hong2023metagpt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)), through conversable agents with human proxies ([wu2023autogen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08155)), to free role-play ([li2023camel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17760)); surveys organise agents into profile, memory, planning and action modules ([wang2023survey](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11704-024-40231-1)) or brain, perception and action ([xi2023rise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07864)). In those terms StripCheck's ingest, retrieval and verifier "agents" are specialised nodes under a deterministic supervisor, not autonomous peers. LangGraph supplies the human-in-the-loop mechanism: `interrupt()` pauses a node, saves state through a checkpointer keyed by a thread identifier and resumes with the user's answer, and because the node restarts from its beginning on resume, work done before the question has to be idempotent ([langchain2026interrupts](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/interrupts)); a SQLite checkpointer suits local deployment and makes runs replayable for evaluation ([langchain2026persistence](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/durable-execution)).

**Small local models need small schemas and a deterministic fallback.** A 2025 position paper argues that small language models are "sufficiently powerful, inherently more suitable, and necessarily more economical" for the narrow, repetitive calls agents make ([belcak2025slm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02153)), but it is an argument rather than a benchmark, and AgentBench found a significant gap between commercial models and open models of 70B parameters or fewer, driven by weak long-horizon reasoning, decision-making and instruction following ([liu2024agentbench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688)). Constrained decoding makes small models' outputs parseable: Outlines enforces regular-expression and grammar constraints through finite-state indexing of the vocabulary ([willard2023outlines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09702)), XGrammar reports up to 100x speed-ups ([dong2025xgrammar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15100)), and Ollama accepts a JSON Schema in its `format` parameter ([ollama2026structured](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/structured-outputs)). JSONSchemaBench shows that engines differ widely on hard real-world schemas, from 0.41 empirical coverage for Guidance to 0.03 for Outlines, while constrained decoding did not reduce an 8B model's task accuracy ([geng2025jsonschemabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10868)). Tam et al. found that strict format restrictions can hurt reasoning (LLaMA-3-8B fell from 74.7 to 48.9 on GSM8K when forced into JSON) while classification-style tasks often improve, and recommend placing reasoning before the answer ([tam2024speak](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-industry.91)). StripCheck's adjudication is closer to classification (choose a candidate or none), but constrained decoding guarantees syntax, not truth: a schema-valid answer can still name the wrong batch, which is why every LLM output is re-validated by deterministic code.

**Evaluation and oversight literature favours reliability, abstention and sparing escalation.** τ-bench scores agents on the end state they leave and introduces pass^k across repeated trials; GPT-4o-class function-calling agents solved fewer than 50% of tasks, with pass^8 below 25% in the retail domain ([yao2024taubench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)). Kapoor et al. argue for joint optimisation of cost and accuracy, proper hold-out sets and standardised harnesses ([kapoor2024agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)); BFCL tests abstention alongside function calling ([patil2025bfcl](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/patil25a.html)); and When2Call tests whether a model should call a tool, ask a follow-up question or say the question cannot be answered, finding considerable room for improvement ([ross2025when2call](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.naacl-long.174)). Vendor-neutral tracing conventions for GenAI spans exist in OpenTelemetry's GenAI semantic-conventions repository ([otel2026genai](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai)) and in OpenInference ([arize2026openinference](https://github.com/Arize-ai/openinference)). Health-domain agent work is converging on the same hybrid pattern but remains thin: MedAgentBench's best model completed 69.67% of 300 physician-written tasks in a virtual EHR ([jiang2025medagentbench](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIdbp2500144)), and 2026 preprints use an LLM only to normalise messy strings while fuzzy rules judge sepsis-bundle compliance ([tripathi2026sepsis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13617)), gate diagnostic hand-off deterministically (49.3% diagnostic precision, 11.3 points above an unconstrained baseline, on 150 simulated cases) ([srivastava2026handoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18068)), or verify LLM-abduced trial matches logically ([qu2026neurosymbolic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20895)). Two 2026 preprints caution about human oversight itself: reviewers agreed only moderately on which agent actions were risky (Fleiss' κ = 0.52 on 125 actions), and modelled safety peaks at an intermediate escalation rate because escalating everything fatigues the human ([turan2026oversight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919)); an audit of 63 public agent artifacts found that validator independence, checkpoint placement and recovery are rarely documented ([shi2026unwatched](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29547)).

**Position for StripCheck.** The literature supports a mostly deterministic LangGraph workflow with a bounded LLM planner and adjudicator, an independent hard-rule verifier, an `interrupt()` question only when a character is genuinely ambiguous, and a deterministic fallback when the local model fails or is unsure. No peer-reviewed health-domain study was found that combines a hard-rule verifier with abstention in a consumer tool; the 2026 neuro-symbolic examples are preprints or workshop papers, so StripCheck should present itself as an instance of an emerging pattern, evaluated on its own data.

### 3.5 Abstention, deferral and wording make CANT_VERIFY and NOT_LISTED design objects, not afterthoughts

**Abstention is a 55-year-old idea that LLMs still fail to practise.** Chow framed the trade-off between recognition error and rejection in 1970 ([chow1970reject](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1970.1054406)); El-Yaniv and Wiener formalised selective classification and its risk-coverage trade-off ([elyaniv2010noisefree](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/el-yaniv10a.html)); and Geifman and El-Yaniv built selective classifiers on deep networks that guarantee a user-specified risk, for example 2% top-5 ImageNet error with probability 99.9% at almost 60% coverage ([geifman2017selective](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html)). Wen et al.'s survey treats abstention, "the refusal of large language models (LLMs) to provide an answer", as a safety mechanism and organises work around the query, the model and human values ([wen2025abstention](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00754)). Yet the ACL 2026 MedQAbstain study finds that "state-of-the-art LLMs systematically overcommit, rarely abstaining even when the question itself is hidden" ([cocchieri2026abstain](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1365)). CANT_VERIFY is a reject option in exactly this sense, and the evidence says it must be enforced by the verifier rather than left to the local LLM's judgement. It should be reported as a risk-coverage pair (coverage as the share of strips given a definite verdict, selective risk as the error rate among them), and the reject region should be cost-sensitive, because a false NOT_LISTED on a listed batch and a false FLAGGED on a genuine one carry different harms.

**Deferring to a human helps only if the human step resists over-reliance.** Learning-to-defer extends rejection to account for the downstream decision-maker, including biased or inconsistent humans ([madras2018defer](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/09d37c08f7b129e96277388757530c72-Abstract.html)), and Mozannar and Sontag learn the classifier and the rejector jointly with a consistent surrogate loss ([mozannar2020defer](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b.html)). The human-factors evidence is sobering. Automation bias produces omission and commission errors in novices and experts alike and is not prevented by training or instructions ([parasuraman2010complacency](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055)); a systematic review of 74 studies of clinical decision support (from 13,821 retrieved) lists design mitigations such as providing information rather than recommendations and updating confidence levels ([goddard2012automation](https://doi.org/10.1136/amiajnl-2011-000089)); AI explanations raised acceptance of AI recommendations "regardless of its correctness" without improving complementary team performance ([bansal2021whole](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445717)); and cognitive forcing functions reduced over-reliance in a 199-participant experiment, although the most effective designs were the least liked ([bucinca2021trust](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287)). For StripCheck the evidence-backed form of the human question is a forced comparison against the physical strip, asked only when a character is genuinely ambiguous (the escalation-fatigue argument of Section 3.4), with its usability cost measured rather than assumed.

**Free-text LLM output is not attributable enough to carry a verdict.** Neural generation is "prone to hallucinate unintended text" ([ji2023hallucination](https://doi.org/10.1145/3571730)), LLMs produce "plausible yet nonfactual content" that retrieval augmentation does not eliminate ([huang2025hallucination](https://doi.org/10.1145/3703155)), and on the ALCE benchmark even the best models lacked complete citation support about 50% of the time on ELI5 questions ([gao2023alce](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.398)). Rashkin et al.'s Attributable to Identified Sources framework gives a way to audit whether each claim traces to a named source ([rashkin2023ais](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00486)). Runtime guardrails exist as programmable rails ([rebedea2023nemo](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-demo.40)) and as learned input-output classifiers ([inan2023llamaguard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06674)), but the learned classifiers target content-moderation taxonomies, not medical over-reassurance such as "this medicine is safe". StripCheck sits at the deterministic end of this spectrum: verdict sentences are templates filled from verified fields and list rows, each citing the list and its month, and banned phrases ("safe", "genuine", "stop taking") act as output rails. Because the LLM never writes the final sentence, the guarantee is structural rather than lexical, which also makes every verdict auditable in the AIS sense.

**A bare negative is read as "no risk", so NOT_LISTED must say what it does not mean.** In an online experiment with 1,200 UK adults, only **54.3%** correctly understood a bare "Your coronavirus test result is negative"; NHS wording raised this to 71.1%, adding "But there is still a chance you may be infectious" raised it to **88.7%** (adjusted odds ratio 3.25 against NHS wording), and an infographic to 90.7%, without changing behavioural intentions ([batteux2022residual](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-056533)). Consumer health tools are error-prone and skew cautious: 23 symptom checkers listed the correct diagnosis first in 34% of vignettes and gave appropriate triage advice in 57% ([semigran2015symptom](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h3480)). A NOT_LISTED result therefore needs an explicit residual-risk sentence ("not found on the lists checked as of this month; this does not confirm the medicine is genuine or of standard quality") and neutral styling. This survey found no study of whether green is read as "safe" in health results, so StripCheck's no-green rule (which Nazar's "never green" convention shares, Section 2.4) is a design rationale to be tested in a user study, not an evidence-based finding.

**Governance places StripCheck on the informational side of the medical-device line, provided its wording stays there.** WHO's six principles for AI in health include protecting autonomy, ensuring safety for defined uses, transparency and accountability ([who2021ethics](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240029200)), and its guidance on large multi-modal models names false or incomplete statements and automation bias among the risks ([who2024lmm](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240084759)). ICMR's 2023 guidelines state that AI "should neither exaggerate nor underestimate the seriousness of the clinical condition" and that human oversight and manual override "must be made compulsory" for AI capable of serious harm ([icmr2023ai](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/Ethical-guidelines/Ethical_Guidelines_AI_Healthcare_2023.pdf)). CDSCO's draft guidance on medical device software (October 2025) defines software as a medical device by a "medical purpose" such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, mitigation, prediction or treatment of disease, and states that "not all software used within healthcare is qualified as a medical device" ([cdsco2025softwaredraft](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Draft%20guidance%20document%20on%20Medical%20Device%20Software%2021%2010%202025.pdf)). A tool that only reports whether a printed batch appears on a regulator's list does not obviously meet that definition, while advice to stop or continue a medicine would move it towards one; this is a reading of a draft, not a regulatory determination. On privacy, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 excludes processing by an individual for personal or domestic purposes ([india2023dpdp](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)), and the DPDP Rules notified on 13 November 2025 phase in substantive obligations by 13 May 2027 ([azb2025dpdprules](https://www.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1708314/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect)); an offline tool that never transmits photos or saved batches is the strongest available position, again subject to legal review.

**Position for StripCheck.** Rule-enforced abstention, a sparing and forced human comparison, template-only verdict text with source citations, residual-risk wording on NOT_LISTED and banned clinical advice are each supported by a separate literature; together they keep the tool informational and auditable.

### 3.6 Building the list: extraction is easy on ruled PDFs, fragile under layout drift, and regulator data is itself dirty

**Rule-based extraction first, learned table models as the fallback.** The only independent comparison found, of ten parsers on DocLayNet pages in six document categories, reports low recall for rule-based tools everywhere except manuals and government tenders (where Camelot reached recall 0.72), with the learned Table Transformer best in four of the six categories ([adhikari2024comparative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09871)). pdfplumber "works best on machine-generated, rather than scanned, PDFs" and lets a parser fix line, text or explicit table strategies per source ([plumber2026readme](https://github.com/jsvine/pdfplumber)); Camelot offers ruled-line and whitespace modes, although its own benchmark is vendor-run ([camelot2026comparison](https://camelot-py.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/comparison.html)). Learned models progressed from TableNet's segmentation of scanned tables ([paliwal2020tablenet](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2019.00029)) through PubTables-1M and its DETR-style Table Transformer, trained on nearly a million tables ([smock2021pubtables](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52688.2022.00459)), to TableFormer, which copies cell text from the PDF's text layer instead of OCR-decoding it and reports TEDS of 91-98.5% on simple and 88.7-95% on complex tables ([nassar2022tableformer](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52688.2022.00457)). Taking digits from the text layer rather than transcribing them is exactly what a batch-number table needs. DocLayNet's 80,863 annotated pages include law, regulation and tender categories, the nearest benchmark to regulator documents ([pfitzmann2022doclaynet](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534678.3539043)); OCR-free and multimodal models such as Donut ([kim2021donut](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-19815-1_29)), LayoutLMv3 ([huang2022layoutlmv3](https://doi.org/10.1145/3503161.3548112)) and Nougat ([blecher2023nougat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13418)) target forms and scientific pages. For a laptop, Docling packages DocLayNet layout analysis and TableFormer under an MIT licence for commodity hardware ([auer2024docling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09869); [livathinos2025docling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17887)), SmolDocling claims that a 256M-parameter VLM competes with models up to 27 times larger ([nassar2025smoldocling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11576)), and PaddleOCR 3.0's PP-StructureV3 offers document parsing from the same family as StripCheck's OCR ([cui2025paddleocr](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.05595)).

**LLM extraction needs span grounding, not just a schema.** Constrained decoding guarantees syntactic validity ([willard2023outlines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09702); [geng2025jsonschemabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10868)) but not fidelity, and strict formats can degrade reasoning ([tam2024speak](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-industry.91)), so diagnosis of what changed in a table is best kept as a free-text step separate from emitting a constrained mapping. Faithfulness requires a grounding check. SafePassage requires every extraction to be backed by an aligned source snippet and reports hallucinations reduced by up to 85% ([barrow2025safepassage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00276)); AXE ties each extracted value to its source DOM node and reaches 88.1% F1 zero-shot with a 0.6B model ([mansour2026axe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01838)); and LangExtract maps every extraction to its exact character interval, leaving unlocatable ones null, although its custom output schemas are not supported for Ollama ([goel2026langextract](https://github.com/google/langextract)). The NSQ tools of Section 2.4 already apply the same rule: Nazar requires every LLM-extracted value to appear in the source text ([rastogi2026nazar](https://github.com/pranshurastogi/Nazar)), and one ingest pipeline drops rows whose batch and drug name are not on the page and checks parsed counts against stated totals ([swahub2026nsq](https://github.com/Swa-hub/nsq-medicine-tracker)).

**A format change is a schema-matching problem with a quarantine exit.** Mapping renamed or reordered headers onto a canonical schema is classic schema matching ([rahm2001survey](https://doi.org/10.1007/s007780100057)). LLM matchers work from names and descriptions but degrade with too little or too much context, and are best used to bootstrap candidates under human or rule verification ([parciak2024schema](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11852)); retrieve-then-rank designs ([sheetrit2024rematch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01567)) and cascades in which small models retrieve and large models rerank ([liu2025magneto](https://doi.org/10.14778/3742728.3742757)) generalise the pattern, and foundation models handle such wrangling tasks without task-specific training ([narayan2022foundation](https://doi.org/10.14778/3574245.3574258)). Practitioner drift handling supplies the policy vocabulary: Delta Lake rejects structural mismatches by default, and Auto Loader can rescue non-conforming data into a separate column rather than silently absorbing it ([msft2026drift](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/implement-manage-data-quality-constraints-unity-catalog/4-detect-manage-schema-drift)). CDSCO's August 2025 move from monthly PDFs to a searchable portal (Section 1.4) is a breaking drift of exactly this kind; a mapping is accepted only if the mapped columns' values pass per-column validators, and otherwise the whole file goes to quarantine with an alert.

**Validation and quarantine are established practice; regulator data is demonstrably dirty.** Data validation as "unit tests for data" runs in production at scale in Google's TFX, used by hundreds of product teams ([breck2019validation](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/928f1160e52192e3e0017fb63ab65391-Abstract.html)), in Amazon's declarative Deequ with incremental validation and constraint suggestion ([schelter2018deequ](https://doi.org/10.14778/3229863.3229867)), and in the open-source Great Expectations ([gx2026core](https://github.com/great-expectations/great_expectations)); the dead-letter channel is the messaging-pattern origin of quarantine ([hohpe2003dlc](https://www.enterpriseintegrationpatterns.com/patterns/messaging/DeadLetterChannel.html)). LLMClean shows a safer division of labour for repair, in which the LLM generates the cleaning rules (ontology functional dependencies) and deterministic code applies them, with results comparable to expert-written rules on three datasets ([biester2024llmclean](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-70421-5_7)). Regulator records need all of this. FDA's own data-quality poster found Responsible Firm identifiers missing in 20% of drug recalls in FY2020 and 29% in FY2022 ([nadeem2023res](https://www.fda.gov/media/168771/download)); raw FDA recall data hold more than 13,000 drug-recall entries representing about 3,000 recall events, and a single 2014 label mix-up recalled by two companies produced 538 entries ([redica2023rawfda](https://www.redica.com/resources/post/is-raw-fda-data-skewing-your-analyses)); and openFDA warns that records are revised after publication, for instance when more lots are added ([openfda2026enforcement](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/enforcement/)). CDSCO's own August 2026 state table contains a rosuvastatin row whose expiry date (June 2025) precedes its manufacturing date (July 2025) ([medicaldialogues2026august](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/cdsco/state-drug-alert-azithromycin-cefixime-among-191-drug-samples-flagged-by-cdsco-for-quality-failures-in-august-180004)). This survey found no peer-reviewed study of automated extraction of recall or alert tables from regulator documents.

**Position for StripCheck.** The literature supports a tiered ingest agent: deterministic parsing of portal JSON and PDF text layers, a learned table model when validation fails, LLM repair only when the repaired value is grounded in the source span and re-passes validators, quarantine with reason codes, and versioned rows that carry the list month and fetch date. Because regulator-table extraction with validation is an unclaimed research niche, StripCheck's quarantine statistics over the 2019-2026 CDSCO corpus would themselves be a reportable result.

## 4. Recall notification stops at the trade, so continuous monitoring has to start from the pack

### 4.1 Recall effectiveness is audited per consignee, and patients hear late and partially

Regulators audit recalls at the level of the firms that received the product, not the patients who hold it. The US recall-strategy rule lists, among its factors, the "degree to which the product remains unused in the market-place", sets recall depth at consumer, retail or wholesale level, defines effectiveness checks from level A (100% of consignees contacted) to level E (none) and reserves public warnings for serious hazards ([fda2026cfr742](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-7/subpart-C/section-7.42)); India's equivalent reaches consumers only for Class I (Section 1.4). The few patient-facing studies show an unsolved last mile. Dutch patients who had lived through a recall knew little about recall procedures, confused recalls with shortages, struggled to judge risk and preferred personalised, pharmacist-led communication proportionate to urgency ([annema2026patient](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11096-025-02082-z)). The 2018 valsartan recall shows both the speed of trade-side action and the cost of disruption: Canadian valsartan dispensing fell 57.8% between June and September 2018 while other angiotensin-receptor blockers rose 14.6% ([fenna2021valsartan](https://doi.org/10.9778/cmajo.20200232)); among 55,461 Ontario patients on recalled valsartan, emergency visits for hypertension rose from 0.11% to 0.17% in the first month ([jackevicius2020valsartan](https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.119.044494); [acc2019valsartan](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2019/11/14/13/36/sat-715am-population-impact-generic-valsartan-recall-aha-2019)); and in US claims data, hypertension patients on valsartan had a higher risk of composite cardiac events (HR 1.22) than those on non-recalled drugs, which the authors link to the recall and the shortages that followed, while adherent heart-failure patients showed no difference ([devine2024arb](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.123.032266); [devine2024valsartan](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.5777)). Direct notification yields only partial response: after 300 veterans were mailed a recall letter there was no change in blood-pressure control, and only 11 of 33 valsartan medication changes were documented as recall-related ([morizio2021veterans](https://doi.org/10.1177/20420986211016173)). The ranitidine withdrawal produced an immediate population-level switch from H2-receptor antagonists to proton-pump inhibitors in Korea ([choi2025ranitidine](https://doi.org/10.3961/jpmph.24.357)), and around Chennai about three quarters of 311 physicians and pharmacists surveyed were aware of the FDA's ranitidine recall ([begam2021ranitidine](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43094-021-00267-7)). Two design rules follow: a FLAGGED verdict should direct the user to a pharmacist or prescriber and never imply stopping a chronic medicine abruptly, and the verdict should distinguish "this batch is listed" from shortage or a product-wide ban.

The closest published analogue of StripCheck's medicine cabinet comes from a US academic medical centre, where a nightly job polled FDA's recall API, matched new recalls to patients' EHR medication lists and sent patient-portal messages. It worked technically, but the authors concluded that automated recall notification cannot be deployed clinically because the EHR prescription cannot be traced to the lot a community pharmacy actually dispensed ([gadgil2026automating](https://doi.org/10.2196/68345)). That missing link is exactly what a photographed strip supplies: the patient's own pack carries the batch number.

### 4.2 Push beats pull, and freshness has to be stated

Vehicle recalls are the best-documented analogue. Even with mandatory first-class mail and a free VIN lookup, 69% of passenger vehicles recalled in 2018 were remedied 15 months after a remedy became available, while manufacturers in the Takata campaign reached almost 80% with at least monthly outreach by certified mail, postcards, email, phone and social media ([gao2024vehicle](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106356)). The VIN lookup rule sets the freshness precedent StripCheck should copy: recall data must be "updated at least once every seven (7) calendar days" with the date of the last update shown on the search and results pages ([ecfr2026vin](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-573/section-573.15)). India's newer push channels all stop at the trade. Telangana's drugs control administration sends bulk SMS to retail pharmacies and wholesalers when its laboratory declares a sample NSQ ([medicaldialogues2026telangana](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/telangana-dca-introduces-sms-alert-system-to-warn-pharmacies-about-substandard-medicines-166301)); Karnataka's real-time portal freezes NSQ stock through about 15,000 stockists and wholesalers, with about 45,000 retailers planned, and its minister acknowledged that under the earlier process substandard medicines could still reach the public ([medicaldialogues2026karnataka](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/karnataka-launches-real-time-portal-to-freeze-nsq-drugs-track-ndps-medicine-sales-170872)); and a 2019 Drugs Consultative Committee sub-committee proposed WhatsApp groups, NSQ websites, a mobile app and a linked portal, with no evidence found that the app or portal was built ([medicaldialogues2019cdscorecall](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/cdsco-committee-to-draft-new-guidelines-for-effective-recall-of-substandard-drugs)). None of these systems has published effectiveness data, and none notifies patients. India's pharmacovigilance programme is a separate channel for adverse-reaction signals aimed at professionals, receiving about 100,000 case reports a year and issuing 56 drug-safety alerts between March 2016 and June 2017, not batch-quality data ([thota2018pvpi](https://doi.org/10.4103/picr.picr_29_17)). WHO's GSMS works through regulators' focal points, contacting them within 72 hours (24 hours where adverse reactions occur) and issuing public Medical Product Alerts only for significant threats ([who2026gsms](https://www.who.int/who-global-surveillance-and-monitoring-system)); a Lancet regional editorial counts seven WHO alerts on contaminated oral liquids since 2022, almost all made in India and linked to over 300 deaths, and calls for "a robust drug recall mechanism" ([lancet2025tiny](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2025.100705)). Elsewhere a regulator's web page is the de facto public alert channel: in Rwanda, 33.0% of recalls from 2019 to 2023 were Class I and India was the top country of manufacture of recalled products (29.2%) ([bahizi2024rwanda](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-024-19245-8)).

### 4.3 Lags mean many listed batches have already been consumed

No published study estimates what fraction of a batch is sold or consumed before an alert, but every component of the delay is documented somewhere. Of 50 substandard-drug recalls posted by Nepal's regulator in 2023-2025, 34% were recalled after 15 months on the market ([barma2025recall](https://doi.org/10.1177/20420986251398725)). Indian courts have found sampling-to-report times far beyond the 60-day limit in the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules: in a Bombay High Court case reported in January 2026, a sample forwarded on 14 July 2022 received its NSQ report on 18 January 2023 and the prosecution was quashed, with the court calling for more laboratories and online monitoring ([livelaw2026ashwani](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/bombay-high-court/bombay-hc-drugs-cosmetics-rules-sample-testing-60-days-web-host-reports-strict-action-erring-officers-520565); [verdictum2026ashwani](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/bombay-high-court/ashwani-v-state-of-maharashtra-2026bhc-nag945-rule-45-drug-testing-delayed-erring-officials-1605430)), and in 2024 the Karnataka High Court noted an analyst who took more than a year, samples tested after their shelf life had expired, and directed the creation of an online system for expeditious testing by Government Analysts and the uploading of their reports ([livelaw2024zim](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/karnataka-high-court/karnataka-high-court-orders-dcgi-implement-online-system-expedited-drug-testing-and-reporting-drugs-and-cosmetics-act-268613)). The monthly list then adds about three weeks ([pioneer2026august](https://dailypioneer.com/news/220-drug-samples-fail-quality-tests-august)); one azithromycin batch in the August 2026 state table was manufactured in October 2024, a month short of two years before it was listed ([medicaldialogues2026august](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/cdsco/state-drug-alert-azithromycin-cefixime-among-191-drug-samples-flagged-by-cdsco-for-quality-failures-in-august-180004)). CDSCO's recall clocks start only after the analyst's report ([cdsco2024recall](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/biologicals/Guidelinerapid24.pdf)), and although its recall log records quantities produced, distributed, unsold and returned ([cdsco2012recall](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/biologicals/4GuidelineRecalRapidAlert.pdf)), no public figures were found. A patient's exposure window is therefore the sum of market residence before sampling (unobserved), sampling to report (statutorily 60 days, often exceeded), report to list (about three weeks) and list to patient (no channel at all for Class II and III). Three consequences for StripCheck follow: re-check saved batches each time a new list is ingested, show the list month and ingest date on every verdict, and phrase NOT_LISTED as "not listed as of this list", never as safe.

### 4.4 Machine-readable feeds exist, while medication apps do not check them and lose their users

openFDA's drug-enforcement endpoint is the machine-readable precedent for CDSCO's JSON: Recall Enterprise System records from 2004, updated weekly, with explicit disclaimers that it should not be used for public alerts or to track a recall's lifecycle, that status is not updated after classification, that recalls may later be expanded to more lots, and "Do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care" ([openfda2026enforcement](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/enforcement/)). Analyses of these data show lists that grow for decades without exposure denominators: 13,248 FDA drug recalls in 2015-2024 ([bekele2026recallclass](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43441-026-01016-9)), 286 opioid recalls covering more than 350 million units with unit counts missing for 11.9% of events ([nunes2026opioid](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.70341)), and nitrosamine recalls from 2018 to 2025 led by angiotensin-receptor blockers and metformin ([yerram2026nitrosamine](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43441-025-00891-y)). Recall infrastructure has documented data-quality weaknesses even in high-income regulators ([hhsoig2017foodrecall](https://oig.hhs.gov/oas/reports/region1/11601502.asp)). Automated monitoring of SF reports needs human curation: the Medicine Quality Monitoring Globe collected 11,000 incidents from news media in five languages between July 2018 and December 2024, but its English classifier's precision was only 37% (recall 90%), so curators review every item before publication ([freifeld2026globe](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001648)). On the consumer side, free medication-adherence apps centre on tracking history, which users also want most, and the reviewed abstract mentions no recall or batch checking ([messner2025adherence](https://doi.org/10.2196/68919)), while app-based chronic-disease interventions lose 43% of users on average (95% CI 29-57%) ([meyerowitzkatz2020dropout](https://doi.org/10.2196/20283)). This survey found no peer-reviewed evaluation of SMS or app-based consumer drug-recall alerts.

**Position for StripCheck.** The medicine cabinet reuses the nightly recall-matching architecture that failed in the EHR setting only for lack of lot data, and supplies the lot from the patient's own strip. Because engagement decays, its value should not depend on the user reopening the app: each list ingest triggers a background re-check of every saved batch, and every verdict carries the list month and ingest date in the manner of the VIN-lookup rule. Measuring the manufacture-to-listing lag across StripCheck's CDSCO corpus (each row carries a manufacturing date and a reporting month) would be the first published estimate of that lag for Indian NSQ batches.

## 5. The Indian user buys over the counter, reads in many languages and decides at the chemist's counter

### 5.1 The chemist's counter is both the point of purchase and the source of advice

Buying medicine without a prescription is mainstream in India. A meta-analysis of 17 Indian studies (10,248 participants) pooled self-medication prevalence at **53.57% (95% CI 36.92-70.21%)**, with heterogeneity so extreme (I² = 100%, study range 8.3-92%) that the figure is only indicative ([rashid2020selfmed](https://doi.org/10.2174/1574884714666191122103953)). In interviews at 15 stand-alone pharmacies in Tamil Nadu, an average of 38% of daily sales were made without a prescription, every pharmacy admitted selling prescription drugs over the counter, seven sold antibiotics without a prescription, and 40% of customers asked by brand name ([chandran2022antibiotics](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_2157_21)). In a web survey of 537 people, 78.6% had heard of generics but 44.9% did not know what type of drugs were on their own prescription ([rathi2021generic](https://doi.org/10.22159/ajpcr.2021.v14i2.40088)). A parallel generic channel is growing fast: the government reports Jan Aushadhi outlets rising from 84 in 2014 to more than 19,200 in 2026 ([ians2026janaushadhi](https://ianslive.in/jan-aushadhi-kendras-grow-to-over-19200-from-just-84-in-2014-govt--20260617182223)). The retail trade is large and politically organised, with the chemists' body claiming to represent 12.4 lakh chemists and distributors when it called a nationwide shutdown on 20 May 2026 against online pharmacy ([etv2026pharmacystrike](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/health/pharmacies-across-india-shut-may-20-epharmacy-concerns-enn26051305103)), whose 2018 draft rules have never been notified as binding regulation ([kumar2026epharmacy](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20552346)). Combined with the brand-name collisions documented in Section 3.3 ([neelakantan2024lookalike](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2024.100425)), this context means StripCheck must read generic names as well as brands, cannot assume users know what they take, and should route its abstain outcome to the pharmacist, who is already the de facto advisor. A read-only checker that neither sells nor dispenses also stays outside the contested e-pharmacy regime.

### 5.2 Phones are near-universal, but access, language and literacy are uneven

India had **886 million active internet users in 2024, 55% of them rural, and 98% of users accessed Indic-language content**; women made up 47% of users and about 58% of rural shared-device users, according to the IAMAI-KANTAR report as covered by PTI ([iamai2025internet](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/01/16/dcm51-biz-iamai-internet-usage-report.html)). Telephone subscriptions reached 1,348.08 million and broadband subscriptions 1,001.50 million (excluding machine-to-machine) at the end of June 2026, although these count connections, not people ([trai2026pr104](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-07/PR_No104of2026_0.pdf)). Gender and rural gaps persist: in a secondary analysis of NFHS-6, the share of women aged 15-49 who had ever used the internet rose from 33.3% to 64.3% and mobile-phone ownership from 54% to 63.6%, with ownership at 57.4% in rural areas against 77.6% in urban ones ([dataful2026womeninternet](https://insights.dataful.in/articles/womens-internet-use-nearly-doubles-but-access-gaps-remain)). No national measure of health literacy was found; in one rural West Bengal community only **11.7% of adults had adequate health literacy** ([reshma2024healthliteracy](https://doi.org/10.22038/jhl.2024.79591.1566)). A systematic review of 22 studies on mHealth adoption in India names the barriers directly: missing local-language systems, technical wording, low literacy and digital skills, women's limited access to their own phones, and privacy on shared devices ([thomas2024mhealth](https://doi.org/10.1093/oodh/oqae046)). The design implications are a multilingual, voice-capable interface with minimal typing, and a medicine cabinet that respects the privacy of a phone shared within a household.

### 5.3 Consumers inspect packaging themselves, and the only formal consumer channel is for side effects

An industry-sponsored online poll of 1,639 urban consumers in nine cities found that 33% had met a counterfeit healthcare product in the past year, that 52% check authenticity before buying, and that 60% trust their own ability to spot a fake; for healthcare products, 66% check packaging quality, 48% look for a hologram and 40% check a QR code ([southfirst2026counterfeit](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/a-fake-medicine-can-look-genuine-survey-finds-one-in-three-consumers-met-a-counterfeit-healthcare-product/)). That self-confidence sits uneasily with the evidence that visual inspection fails (only 8% of aware Jordanians identified SF medicines correctly, [falah2025prevalence](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2024-018189)) and that copied codes scan perfectly (Section 2.1). It does, however, show that camera-based checking and QR scanning are already familiar habits. The only formal consumer reporting channel found is the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India, which accepts adverse-reaction reports through a consumer form, a toll-free helpline and an Android app ([ipc2018pvpifaq](https://www.ipc.gov.in/PvPI/faq.html)); in November 2025 the DCGI asked states to display the PvPI QR code and helpline at every pharmacy, calling under-reporting a long-standing problem ([outlook2025pvpiqr](https://www.outlookindia.com/amp/story/healthcare-spotlight/dcgi-asks-states-to-mandate-display-of-pvpi-qr-code-at-all-pharmacies)). These channels are for side effects, not suspected spurious batches. This survey found no study of what Indian consumers do when they suspect a bad medicine, nor of how far they trust their chemist, which makes a user study a natural part of StripCheck's evaluation.

### 5.4 Design evidence favours pictures, voice, minimal typing and a human back-up

Classic HCI-for-development work found text interfaces unusable for first-time low-literacy users and error-prone for literate novices, and recommends graphical cues, voice annotation, local-language support in both text and audio, shallow navigation, avoiding non-numeric text input and integrating human mediators ([medhi2011mobile](https://doi.org/10.1145/1959022.1959024)); illiterate domestic workers in Bangalore strongly preferred text-free designs with voice feedback and a consistent help feature ([medhi2007textfree](https://doi.org/10.1162/itid.2007.4.1.37)). Pictograms help but must be tested locally: among 90 low-literacy adults in South Africa, mean comprehension of ten medicine pictograms was 7.9 of 10, two pictograms failed the ISO 66.7% criterion, and only 15.6% of participants had adequate health literacy ([dowse2023pictograms](https://doi.org/10.4102/hsag.v28i0.2192)). WCAG 2.2, a W3C Recommendation since 12 December 2024, supplies testable criteria relevant to a camera app, such as minimum target size, no redundant re-entry of information and consistent help ([w3c2024wcag22](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)); India's GIGW 3.0, which applies to government websites and apps and draws on WCAG 2.1 and the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016, is the national benchmark rather than a requirement for StripCheck ([nic2026gigw3](https://guidelines.india.gov.in/scope-and-objective/)). India's national digital-health stack is record-centred, with more than 93 crore ABHA identifiers and more than 104 crore linked health records by mid-2026 ([drishti2026abha](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/ayushman-bharat-health-accounts-abha)), and has no batch-quality layer, so an offline checker sits outside it and needs no integration.

**Position for StripCheck.** The verdict screen should combine an icon, one plain sentence in the user's chosen language and a spoken version, never relying on colour or icon alone; the human question should be answered by tapping a candidate rather than typing; and every abstention should end with a referral to the pharmacist and, for suspected reactions, the PvPI helpline. Each of these is a design inference from adjacent evidence rather than a tested result for medicine-alert verdicts, so comprehension testing with Indian users belongs in the evaluation.

## 6. Evaluation has to measure the synthetic-to-real gap, pair the comparisons and score abstention honestly

### 6.1 Rendered packs are a controlled probe, and the synthetic-to-real gap is a result to report

Rendered text is a legitimate and cheap source of evaluation data. MJSynth randomised over 1,400 fonts, colouring, projective distortion, noise, blur and JPEG artefacts, and synthetic-only training reached 99.2% on IC03 and 96.1% on SVT with a 50-word lexicon, with the colouring layer alone worth about 40 points in ablation ([jaderberg2014synthetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2227)); SynthText showed that placing text consistently with scene depth and surface orientation matters more than sheer volume ([gupta2016synthtext](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR.2016.254)). Comparisons are fragile, however: Baek et al. showed that inconsistent training and evaluation sets confounded published scene-text results, and that fine-tuning on real data helped in-distribution while hurting out-of-distribution accuracy ([baek2019wrong](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2019.00481)). The gap to hard real text is large: on Union14M, 13 synthetic-trained recognisers lost **20.50 points on average** (CRNN fell from 78.14 to 57.96) ([jiang2023union14m](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV51070.2023.01878)). Physical-world protocols exist to bridge the tiers. Kurakin et al. printed pages at 600 dpi with four corner QR codes, photographed them with a phone, rectified them from the corner codes and reported the share discarded for undetected corners (at most 10%, typically 3-6%) ([kurakin2017physical](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02533)); MIDV-2020 photographed mock identity documents carrying synthetic persons, a privacy-safe precedent for printing mock strips with controlled batch numbers ([bulatov2022midv2020](https://doi.org/10.18287/2412-6179-CO-1006)); and SmartDoc-QA logged capture conditions such as lighting, blur and perspective for every smartphone image ([nayef2015smartdocqa](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2015.7333960)). StripCheck's evaluation therefore needs three tiers, rendered packs with exact labels, the same packs printed and photographed, and unscripted real strips, with the gap between tiers reported as a finding; the 20-point Union14M drop is a cautionary prior, not an estimate for foil and blister text.

### 6.2 Robustness is a severity sweep per pipeline stage

ImageNet-C fixed the template: 15 corruptions in noise, blur, weather and digital families at five severities (75 variants), used for testing only ([hendrycks2019benchmarking](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12261)). Applied to text, a study of 15,840 degraded page images found errors rising steeply near maximum severity, with perspective warp hurting locally hosted 7B VLMs far more than a large hosted model ([urbanski2025quantifying](https://doi.org/10.5220/0013462800003967)), and among 19 corruption types VLM text understanding deteriorated most under blur and snow ([usama2025vlmcorruptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13690)). For StripCheck that means sweeping blur, glare, perspective, noise and compression at about five levels and reporting, at each level, accuracy, abstention rate and the rescue rate of each stage (QR, OCR, enhanced re-read, VLM). Glare on foil has almost no recognition-specific evidence in the sources found, so StripCheck's measurements would fill a gap rather than repeat one.

### 6.3 Paired tests and small-sample intervals, clustered by pack

Comparing StripCheck with a plain exact-lookup baseline on the same images calls for McNemar's test on discordant pairs ([mcnemar1947note](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02295996)), with Edwards's continuity correction when discordant counts are small ([edwards1948note](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02289261)); Dietterich showed that for algorithms that can be run only once, McNemar's test is the one with acceptable type I error ([dietterich1998approximate](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017197)). Per-arm proportions need Wilson score intervals ([wilson1927probable](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1927.10502953)): exact intervals are too conservative and Wald intervals too narrow, while Wilson's stays near nominal coverage even for very small samples ([agresti1998approximate](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.1998.10480550)), and Wald coverage is "chaotic" enough that Brown, Cai and DasGupta recommend Wilson or Jeffreys intervals for small n ([brown2001interval](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1009213286)). Bootstrap intervals cover metrics without closed forms ([efron1993bootstrap](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-4541-9)), but recent work on LLM evaluation warns against naive use: clustered standard errors can be up to three times the naive ones, paired analysis cuts variance (by a third at a correlation of 0.5 in Miller's example), and a power analysis should precede the evaluation ([miller2024errorbars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)); across 80,000 simulated datasets, nominal 95% CLT intervals covered only 92.5% at N = 100 while Wilson and Bayesian intervals stayed near nominal ([bowyer2025clt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01747)). Several photos of the same pack are not independent, so the pack, not the photo, is the resampling unit.

### 6.4 Abstaining agents need risk-coverage metrics, audited scorers, cost reporting and a frozen plan

With CANT_VERIFY, StripCheck is a selective classifier, so coverage and selective risk ([elyaniv2010noisefree](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/el-yaniv10a.html); [geifman2017selective](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html)) belong next to raw accuracy. Traub et al. propose AUGRC, the average risk of undetected failures, as a replacement summary for the area under the risk-coverage curve, and show that it changes method rankings on five of six datasets across 13 confidence scores ([traub2024augrc](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/047c84ec50bd8ea29349b996fc64af4b-Abstract.html)). Agent-evaluation critiques add three requirements. Simple retry and escalation baselines matched complex agents on HumanEval while costs differed by almost two orders of magnitude, so cost-accuracy Pareto reporting and hold-out sets are needed ([kapoor2024agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)); scorer and task-setup flaws, such as counting empty responses as success, misstated agent performance by up to 100% in relative terms ([zhu2025agentic](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f316275b44ee2de533102913828a8107-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)), which for StripCheck means a scorer that can never reward blanket CANT_VERIFY; and repeated trials with pass^k expose the inconsistency of stochastic planners ([yao2024taubench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)). Pre-registration separates prediction from postdiction by fixing hypotheses, exclusions and tests in advance ([nosek2018preregistration](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)), and machine-learning venues have trialled protocol-first review ([bertinetto2020preregistration](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2020/workshop/16158)); a dated, hashed analysis plan committed before the test set is opened gives StripCheck the same protection. The headline safety metric should separate false reassurance (NOT_LISTED or a wrong batch on a listed pack) from false alarms (FLAGGED on a genuine pack), each with a Wilson interval, and each component (QR, OCR, re-read, VLM, variant search, corroboration, verifier, human question) should be ablated against the full system with paired tests. No source gives an acceptable false-reassurance threshold for medicine screening, so StripCheck must justify its own from a risk analysis.

### 6.5 Usability scales measure ease of use, not safe understanding

The System Usability Scale is a free ten-item measure of perceived usability ([brooke1996sus](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781498710411-35)); Lewis's review reports average reliability around 0.91, a curved grading scale on which 68 is a C, a mean of 77.7 for popular mobile apps, and agreement with full-sample conclusions from about 12 participants ([lewis2018sus](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2018.1455307)). The Mobile App Rating Scale gives expert raters 23 items across engagement, functionality, aesthetics, information and subjective quality, with total-score ICC 0.79 and alpha 0.90 on 50 apps ([stoyanov2015mars](https://doi.org/10.2196/mhealth.3422)), and is the instrument Ciapponi et al. used to rate SF-detection apps (Section 2.3). Neither scale measures whether a user correctly understands that NOT_LISTED is not "safe", the failure Batteux et al. quantified for negative test results ([batteux2022residual](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-056533)), so a verdict-comprehension task belongs alongside SUS in any user study.

**Position for StripCheck.** A defensible evaluation uses three image tiers, a corruption sweep reported per stage, McNemar comparisons against the exact-lookup baseline with Wilson and pack-clustered intervals, risk-coverage and AUGRC reporting with false reassurance as the headline harm, cost and latency on the target laptop, repeated runs of the LLM planner, a frozen analysis plan, and a user study pairing SUS with verdict comprehension. None of the surveyed NSQ tools or packaging-OCR papers reports an evaluation of this kind on real photographed Indian strips.

## 7. Ten open gaps: five StripCheck fills by design, five its evaluation can fill, and three it cannot

The gaps fall into three groups. Design gaps are questions no existing system answers, which StripCheck answers by construction. Empirical gaps are measurements nobody has published, which StripCheck's evaluation can supply. Limits are problems no list-lookup tool can solve, which the paper should state rather than leave for reviewers to find.

Three observations shape how these gaps should be claimed. First, the design gaps are real only as a combination: each element of G1 has a precedent somewhere in Section 2.4, and G4 and G5 are framings that the sources do not state but also do not contradict, so the paper should say "to our knowledge, among the tools found as of October 2026" and cite the closest prior art explicitly. Second, the empirical gaps are where StripCheck can make claims that survive the rapid churn of GitHub projects: a dataset, a confusion table, a calibration curve, a collision rate and a lag distribution remain contributions even if another checker adds photo input next month. Third, the limits are not weaknesses peculiar to StripCheck; they follow from the evidence that substandard quality is only visible to laboratories (Section 1.2) and that identifiers prove provenance at best (Section 2.1), and stating them plainly is what keeps the tool's NOT_LISTED verdict from becoming the false reassurance the risk-communication literature warns about.

## 9. Conclusion

The survey changes what StripCheck should claim to be. The burden literature says the important question in India is not "is this medicine fake?" but "has a laboratory already failed this batch?", because substandard product dominates and only testing reveals it. The verification literature says no existing channel delivers that answer to the person holding the pack, and the component literatures say the hard part is not recognition accuracy but the join, where a confident one-character misread against a closed list produces either a false hit or a false all-clear. StripCheck is therefore best presented as a delivery mechanism for laboratory results with error control at every join. That framing has a consequence the OCR literature alone would miss: because almost every strip a consumer photographs will be unlisted, even a small per-scan rate of false FLAGGED verdicts from batch collisions can outnumber the true ones (a base-rate inference, not a measured result), so the evaluation has to be judged on false reassurance and false alarms at realistic base rates, with abstention scored honestly, rather than on character accuracy.

The second implication concerns durability. The open NSQ tools appeared within about a year of CDSCO's searchable portal and will keep changing, so any feature-level novelty claim is perishable; the dataset, the batch-code confusion table, the calibration curve, the collision rate and the manufacture-to-listing lag are not. Those measurements also point upstream. CDSCO's own guideline aspires to reach the public "by SMS or through website", courts have directed online systems for faster testing and published reports, and the cheapest large improvement in consumer safety would be a versioned, machine-readable feed with consistent batch formatting and test dates. StripCheck's error and quarantine statistics are precisely the evidence needed to argue for that feed.
